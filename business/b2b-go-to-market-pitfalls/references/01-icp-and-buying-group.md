# b2b-go-to-market-pitfalls §1 — Ideal customer and buying group

## 1.1 A profile that excludes
1. **Describe the customer by traits that exclude companies**: size range, sector, tools in use, a trigger event, a
   problem they already pay to solve. Symptom: a profile that fits any company. Check: name three companies that
   fit and three that do not, and say why.
2. **List disqualifiers**: needs the product cannot meet, regulatory limits, a deal size below the cost to serve,
   a buyer with no budget owner. A team that cannot say no sells to whoever replies.
3. **Separate the company from the person.** The profile of the account and the roles inside it are two things; do
   not describe a persona and call it a market.
4. **State the problem they have today and what they do instead** (a competitor, a spreadsheet, nothing). That is
   the real comparison (`business/product-marketing` §1).

## 1.2 Evidence
1. **Build the profile from deals**: the best customers, the lost deals and the ones that churned, with the reason
   in the customer's words. Symptom: a profile built from the founder's network. Fix: a table of recent deals with
   outcome and reason.
2. **Label each trait as observed or assumed.** Do not claim that a sector converts better until the counts have
   been read, and say how few deals the counts rest on.
3. **Check the profile against the product's real limits** with engineering and support, not only against the
   pitch.
4. **Re-run the profile at set intervals**; a market moves. Record the date and the data it used.

## 1.3 The buying group
1. **Name the roles in a purchase**: who feels the problem, who evaluates, who pays, who can block (security,
   legal, IT). Symptom: a deal that stalls at a review nobody expected. Fix: a role list per segment with what each
   needs to say yes.
2. **Prepare the material each role asks for** (security answers, a cost case, an integration note) before the
   deals need it.
3. **Do not treat the user as the buyer or the reverse.** Say who the message is for.
4. **Ask sales which questions repeat** and put the answers in the material rather than rediscover them per deal.
