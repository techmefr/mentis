# b2b-go-to-market-pitfalls §2 — Launch readiness and channels

## 2.1 Readiness gate
1. **Ask three questions before fixing a date**: does it do one clearly defined thing, would a target buyer choose
   it over their current way, is that one thing a complete experience with no obvious holes? Symptom: a launch
   date set first and the scope cut to match. Fix: a go or no-go owner and a written answer to each.
2. **Name the two failure modes**: polishing in private with nothing shipped, and always adding one more feature.
   Each has a different cure: ship what passes the gate, or cut scope until something does.
3. **Check what the launch depends on**: documentation, onboarding, pricing, support cover, security answers,
   billing. A missing piece is a launch blocker, not a fix for later.
4. **Launch in stages when feedback is cheap**: internal users, a few friendly customers, then wider. State what you
   expect to learn at each stage.

## 2.2 Channels by what you own
1. **Sort channels into owned (email list, site, product, community), rented (social platforms, marketplaces) and
   borrowed (partners, speaking, other people's audiences).** Symptom: all effort on a platform the company does
   not control. Fix: one or two per type, with rented and borrowed channels leading back to an owned one.
2. **Pick channels where your buyer already looks**, from deal evidence (§1.2), not from what competitors do.
3. **One owner and one measure per channel.** A channel with no owner is not a channel.
4. **Platform rules and formats change**; keep them out of the plan and check them when the work happens
   (`skills/source-freshness`).

## 2.3 Enablement and handoff
1. **Brief sales, customer success and support before the public announcement**: what it is, who it is for, what
   it does not do, how to answer the likely objections, where to send questions.
2. **Agree what happens to a lead**: who answers, within what time, with what material, and how it is recorded
   in the CRM (`business/outbound-sales-and-crm-hygiene-pitfalls`). Symptom: sign-ups no one contacted.
3. **Give sales a short list of claims they may make** and the ones they may not (`business/product-marketing` §2). The
   enablement skill asks that a why-we-win claim rest on evidence (win and loss interviews, call recordings, customer
   quotes) or be shown as a hypothesis, that competitor statements be dated and sourced, and that the playbook for a new
   product include buyer profile, qualification, discovery questions, objections and demo flow.
4. **Keep one dated plan** with owners per task and a single place for changes. A plan in four documents has four
   different dates.
5. **Plan the announcement text with the release team** (`business/release-communication`); do not announce what
   has not shipped.
6. **Write the lead rules down.** A revenue-operations skill in the same repository defines a qualified lead by fit and
   engagement together, sets a response window and a qualify-or-reject window between marketing and sales, sends rejected
   leads to a nurture list with a reason code, names a fallback owner so unassigned leads do not go cold, and logs each
   routing decision. Scoring pitfalls it names: over-weighting content downloads, no negative scoring, scores never
   revisited. Take the windows from your own data; none is stated here.
