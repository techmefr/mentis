# sustainability-esg

Business layer (`business/README.md`). The same mechanism `business/product-marketing` applies to a
performance claim in a pitch deck applies here to an environmental or social claim: **a checkable
statement gets checked before it's published, or it isn't published as a fact.** The specific failure
mode this block exists to prevent is greenwashing — a claim that's inflated, selective, or symbolic
relative to what the underlying data actually supports.

**Not a substitute for a qualified ESG/sustainability consultant.** Whether specific disclosure is
legally required, which framework applies, and what a material issue actually is for this business are
questions for that person, not this checklist.

## When
Before publishing a sustainability/ESG report, a public environmental or social commitment, a
certification claim, or any marketing copy that makes an ESG-flavoured statement ("carbon neutral",
"ethically sourced", "diverse workforce").

## Steps

### 1. Start from materiality, not a generic checklist
1. **Identify which ESG issues actually matter to this specific business** before reporting on anything
   — a materiality assessment tied to the actual sustainability model of the business, not a copied list
   of issues that matter to a different industry. Reporting exhaustively on irrelevant issues while
   under-covering the real ones is itself a form of selective disclosure.
2. **Prioritise by where the business genuinely has impact or exposure**, and say so explicitly — a
   short stated rationale for why an issue was included or excluded is what makes the material list
   defensible later.

### 2. Every claim needs a source, same as any other public claim
1. **A quantified claim needs the data and methodology behind it, named**, not just asserted. "We
   reduced emissions by X%" without a stated baseline, boundary (which operations, which scope) and
   methodology is exactly the shape regulators and watchdogs flag as greenwashing.
2. **A qualitative commitment ("committed to sustainability") with no quantified backing anywhere is
   weaker evidence than it reads**, and reads as symbolic exactly because it is — if there's no number
   behind it yet, say that it's a direction, not an achievement.
3. **Partial quantification disclosed as if it were complete is a specific greenwashing pattern** —
   quantifying the flattering part of an impact and describing the rest only qualitatively. State the
   boundary of what was actually measured.
4. **Centralise the underlying data** (emissions, workforce, supplier inputs, governance indicators)
   somewhere every published claim can be traced back to, the same traceability discipline
   `business/data-analytics` §4.3 states for any reported number.

### 3. Route the framework and obligation questions
Take these to a qualified ESG/sustainability consultant or legal, rather than assuming: which
disclosure framework applies (and whether it's mandatory or voluntary for this company's size and
jurisdiction), what counts as material under that framework specifically, and what happens if a past
public commitment isn't met on schedule.

## Output / checkpoint
No pipeline checkpoint (business layer, see `business/README.md`). What it owes: a stated materiality
rationale before reporting, every quantified claim traceable to its data/methodology/boundary, no
qualitative commitment presented as an achievement, and the framework/obligation questions routed to a
qualified person.

## Guardrails
- Never publish a quantified ESG claim with no stated methodology or boundary behind it.
- Never present a qualitative commitment as if it were a measured achievement.
- Never quantify only the flattering part of an impact while describing the rest vaguely — state the
  boundary of what was actually measured.
- Never decide alone which disclosure framework applies or whether disclosure is mandatory — route it.
- This block has no dedicated in-house ESG/sustainability expertise behind it yet: a solid set of
  defaults against greenwashing, not proven doctrine, to be confronted with a real reporting cycle.
