# content-brief-writing-pitfalls §2 — Brief contents and review

## 2.1 What the brief holds
1. **Reader, question, expected action after reading.** Symptom: a piece that informs and leaves the reader with no
   next step.
2. **The one sentence of added value** from §1.3, and the owner page for the query from §1.2.
3. **Outline by questions**, not by keyword blocks: each heading is a question the reader has, in the order they
   would ask it. Mark which question the opening must answer.
4. **Sources the writer must use and sources they must not** (competitor claims, unreviewed statistics). Give links
   and dates.
5. **Product claims allowed and forbidden**, pointing to the claim review (`business/product-marketing` §2).
6. **Voice and exclusions**: terms to use, terms to avoid, internal names to strip, topics the owner has not cleared.
7. **Format and length as a range with a reason**, not a target to pad towards.

## 2.2 Facts, claims and generated drafts
1. **Every figure in a draft carries its source and date**, filled in by the writer, checked by the reviewer.
   Symptom: a precise number nobody can trace.
2. **A generated draft is a draft**: the brief says so and the reviewer treats titles, descriptions, quotes and
   links as unverified until checked (`business/ai-prose-tells`).
3. **No invented experts, quotes or customer stories.** If the brief asks for a quote, name the person who gave it.
4. **Check the links in the draft** resolve and say what the sentence claims.

## 2.3 Definition of done and review
1. **Write the done criteria in the brief**: answers the question, the added value is visible in the first screen,
   claims sourced, links placed, metadata written, reviewer signed.
2. **Name the reviewer who can check the facts**, not only the editor of style. Symptom: a polished piece with a
   wrong claim.
3. **Review against the brief, not against taste.** List what differs from it; the owner decides whether the brief
   or the draft changes.
4. **Record the measure that will judge the piece and the date**, with its conditions (page, period, source of the
   data). Do not set a number the team has no baseline for.
