# content-brief-writing-pitfalls §3 — Refresh and prune

## 3.1 Decay
1. **Mark each page with the facts that expire**: prices, versions, regulations, screenshots, statistics. Symptom: a
   guide that cites a retired product version. Fix: put the expiry in the page record and review on a schedule.
2. **Show a visible updated date only when something changed**, and say what changed. A touched date with no change
   misleads readers. The search guidance names changing a date to look fresh without substantial change as a warning
   sign, asks that the visible and structured dates match, and reserves the last-updated date for significant changes.
3. **Rank what to refresh by value and by risk**: pages that carry a claim about compliance, security or price come
   first.

## 3.2 Merge, redirect, remove
1. **Decide per page: keep, update, merge, redirect or remove.** Write the reason. Symptom: hundreds of thin pages
   no one owns.
2. **When merging, keep the strongest page's address and redirect the others to it**; move the unique content
   across and update the internal links. The redirect guidance read says to use a permanent redirect only when the move is
   final, to send removed pages to a relevant replacement, and that server-side redirects are the most reliably
   understood (Google Search Central, redirects).
3. **Remove only with a check on links in and traffic in**; a page with a real audience may need a replacement,
   not deletion.
4. **Do not rewrite a page that answers its question.** A refresh that changes the angle resets what worked.

## 3.3 Measure honestly
1. **Compare like with like**: same period length, same page, same source, and say what else changed (a redesign,
   a campaign).
2. **A change after a refresh is not a proof of cause.** Say what was measured and what was not.
3. **Keep the brief with the page** so the next refresh starts from the stated reader and question.
