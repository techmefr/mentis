# content-brief-writing-pitfalls

Business layer (`business/README.md`), communication. A brief is the cheapest place to stop a bad piece: after
writing, the cost of a wrong angle is a rewrite. This block is the check on a brief and on a plan of briefs.
Where the material comes from and the hook discipline are `business/content-creation`; editorial trust signals on a
page are `business/content-creation` reference 01; technical search work is `skills/seo`; claims in the copy are
`business/product-marketing` §2.

## When
- A topic or keyword has been chosen and a writer, human or generated, is about to be briefed.
- A content calendar or a topic cluster is planned.
- Existing pages are reviewed for refresh, merge or removal.

## Steps

**Read only the section the task meets.** §1 before choosing the topic; §2 when the brief is written; §3 for the
pages already published.

| § | Covers | Read it when | File |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Reader, intent and overlap: who asks the question, what answer they expect, which existing page already owns it, information gain | a topic is selected or added to a plan | [`01-intent-overlap-and-gain.md`](./references/01-intent-overlap-and-gain.md) |
| 2 | The brief itself: contents of a good one, sources, claims, structure, definition of done, review | a writer is briefed | [`02-brief-contents-and-review.md`](./references/02-brief-contents-and-review.md) |
| 3 | Refresh and prune: dated facts, decay, merge and redirect, measurement that names its conditions | published pages are reviewed | [`03-refresh-and-prune.md`](./references/03-refresh-and-prune.md) |

## Output / checkpoint
No pipeline checkpoint (business layer). What it owes: per piece, a brief with the reader, the question, the
single page that owns the query, the one sentence of added value, the sources, the claims needing a check, the
definition of done and a named reviewer; per plan, a map of queries to pages with no query on two pages.

## Guardrails
- Name the reader and the question before the keyword. Never brief a piece from a keyword alone (§1).
- Check what already exists on the site first. Never publish a second page for a query a page already owns without
  a stated difference (§1).
- Write the added-value sentence before commissioning. Never commission the eleventh copy of the same answer (§1).
- Put the sources and the claims to verify in the brief. Never leave facts for the writer to invent (§2).
- State what done means and who reviews. Never accept a draft with no reviewer who can check its facts (§2).
- Date every fact that decays and review it on a schedule. Never leave an old page to carry a stale number (§3).
- Never promise a ranking or a traffic figure in a brief or a plan; report what was measured, with its conditions.
- This block is partly built from an MIT source, partly checked against search documentation facts, and partly from
  general practice; never run by us on a real plan. Status and open points are in [`references/origin.md`](./references/origin.md).
