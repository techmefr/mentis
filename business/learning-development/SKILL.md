# learning-development

Business layer (`business/README.md`). Training is one of the easiest business spends to justify on
paper and hardest to justify with evidence — a well-attended workshop with high satisfaction scores can
still change nothing about how anyone actually works. This block is the discipline that keeps L&D tied
to a real gap and a checkable outcome.

## When
Proposing, choosing between, or reviewing a training/upskilling program, a certification budget, or a
skills-development initiative.

## Steps

### 1. Start from a real gap, not an available course
1. **Identify the gap before choosing the training**, not the other way round. A survey, manager
   observation, or a concrete recurring mistake/bottleneck traced to a missing skill all beat "this
   course looked useful."
2. **Tie the program to a business objective it's meant to move** — a KPI, a bottleneck, a capability
   the team needs for something already planned. A program with no stated objective has no way to be
   judged afterward except by how it felt.
3. **Establish a baseline before the program starts.** Without a "before" number (time-to-proficiency,
   defect rate, whatever the program targets), there's no way to honestly claim an "after" improvement —
   only an impression that something got better.

### 2. Measure something other than attendance and satisfaction
1. **Attendance and a satisfaction survey measure whether people showed up and liked it, not whether
   anything changed.** Neither is a rejection of measuring them — they're just not evidence of impact on
   their own.
2. **Track skills actually gained** (a certification, a demonstrated competency, a manager sign-off on a
   specific capability) against the gap identified in §1, not against the training's own quiz.
3. **Where the program targets a real outcome, measure that outcome directly**: time-to-proficiency for
   a new skill, career mobility into a role that needed it, a bottleneck's throughput before and after.
   The specific metric follows from what the program was for (§1.2) — there's no universal one.

### 3. Close the loop
1. **Report back against the baseline, not against how the sessions felt.** A program judged only by
   its own survey is grading its own homework.
2. **A program with no measurable movement on its stated objective after a fair evaluation window is a
   signal to change or stop it**, not to run it again because people enjoyed it.

## Output / checkpoint
No pipeline checkpoint (business layer, see `business/README.md`). What it owes: a stated gap and
objective before the program is chosen, a baseline measurement taken before it starts, and an evaluation
against that baseline rather than against attendance or satisfaction alone.

## Guardrails
- Never choose a training program before naming the gap it's meant to close.
- Never report a program's success using only attendance or satisfaction scores.
- Never skip the baseline measurement — without it, "it helped" is unfalsifiable.
- This block has no dedicated in-house L&D expertise behind it yet: a solid set of structural defaults,
  not proven doctrine, to be confronted with a real training program.
