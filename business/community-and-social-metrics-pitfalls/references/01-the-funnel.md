# community-and-social-metrics-pitfalls §1 — The funnel

Four questions, asked in order. A later question is only worth asking once the earlier one has an answer
(GitHub opensource.guide, "Open Source Metrics", CC-BY-4.0).

## 1.1 Discovery
1. **Are people finding it?** Count visitors and where they come from, so you can tell whether promotion
   works (opensource.guide, Discovery).
2. **Stars and similar signs only show that people noticed**; they do not necessarily match downloads or
   use (same section).

## 1.2 Usage
1. **Are people doing something?** Downloads, clones and similar counts. Each source defines a download
   differently, and a download is not necessarily an install or use (opensource.guide, Usage).
2. **Low usage against high discovery has two possible causes:** you are failing to convert the audience, or
   you are attracting the wrong one (same section).
3. **Where the audience comes from changes the expected conversion.** A spike from a mass-audience site
   converts worse than a feature at an event for the target audience (same section).
4. **Own guidance:** to tell the two causes apart, ask a few people from the target audience to react to the
   page, as the source suggests, before changing it.

## 1.3 Retention
1. **Do people contribute and come back?** Popular is not supported: usage can grow while the people who help
   do not (opensource.guide, Retention).
2. **An inflow of new contributors is needed** because active ones move on (same section).
3. **Count first-time, casual and repeat contributors together**, so a stagnant inflow shows (same section).
   Where "casual" starts is up to you; define it first (see part 2).
4. **Other numbers the source lists:** total contributors and commits each, open and newly opened issues and
   requests, and the types of contribution, including comments and documentation.
5. **Own guidance:** for a brand community, read "contributor" as whoever answers, creates or helps in public,
   and state the definition.

## 1.4 Maintainer activity
1. **Are we responding?** Unresponsive maintainers become a bottleneck, and a person who never hears back may
   leave (opensource.guide, Maintainer activity).
2. **Time to respond is the measure.** A response does not need an action; promising a look within a week
   counts (same section).
3. **Stage times can be measured too:** how long an issue stays open, how many close through a fix, whether
   stale ones close, how long a change takes to merge (same section).
4. **Own guidance:** the same four questions work for a social account: reach, clicks or saves, returning
   people, and how fast we answer.
