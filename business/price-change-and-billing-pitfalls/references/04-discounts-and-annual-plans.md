# price-change-and-billing-pitfalls §4 — Discounts and annual plans

A discount is a decision that outlives the conversation that created it. Each one sets an expectation for the
renewal and for the next customer who hears of it.

## 4.1 A discount has four properties
1. **A floor**: the lowest price the person granting it may accept, set by the owner of pricing, not by the
   seller closing the deal.
2. **An owner**: a named person who approved it, and who can end it.
3. **An expiry**: a date or an event after which it no longer applies.
4. **A renewal price**: the price the account returns to when it ends, written at the time it is granted.
A discount given to close a deal and not written down becomes the price the customer expects at renewal.

## 4.2 Annual plans
An annual plan is a trade: the company receives cash earlier and a longer commitment, the customer pays less per
month. Decide the trade on purpose:
1. **What the customer gets**: how much less, and compared with which monthly price (the one that applies today,
   not a retired one).
2. **What happens on early cancellation**: refund, partial refund or none, said before purchase on the page and
   in the order. The billing mechanics of a refund or credit are §5.
3. **What happens at renewal**: which price applies, how long before it the customer is told (§3), and whether
   the discount carries over.
4. **What happens on a mid-term upgrade**: how the unused time is credited, see §5.

## 4.3 What not to assume
Do not state that customers who ask for a discount leave more often: that claim came without data in the source
and has to be read in your own cohort, with the plan, the segment and the acquisition channel held apart. Do not
set the size of the annual discount from an article: it follows from the cost of money, the churn you avoid and
the trade you decided in §4.2.

## 4.4 Metrics
A discounted price changes what "average revenue" means. Say in the report which figure is list and which is
paid (`skills/saas-metric-definition-pitfalls`).
