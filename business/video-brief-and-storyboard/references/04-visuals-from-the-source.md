# video-brief-and-storyboard §4 — Visuals from the source material

> Part 4 of `business/video-brief-and-storyboard`. Read it when frames are being filled with pictures or props.
> Sources, read 2026-10-20 in the clone: the story-spine reference (its final rule) and the capture step of the
> product workflow of an Apache-2.0 HTML-to-video framework. Examples are ours. Sentences marked "own
> guidance" are ours. Not marketing advice (`business/README.md`).

## 4.1 Mine the source before inventing
1. **When the video comes from something concrete (a product page, a change, an article, a data set), read it
   for the pictures before drawing any.** The source says its phrases, entities, verbs and recurring images
   are the props, and that each frame's key picture should be traceable to a specific line of it, the same
   way the frame's why is traced to the message (§3.1).
2. **Prefer the real thing to the generic.** The source's three examples: the real file names over a generic
   file icon, the product's own numbers over invented ones, the article's own metaphor over a stock one.
   Own example: a video about a billing change shows the real invoice layout with the real field that
   changed, not a generic receipt icon with a dollar sign.
3. **Write a source line per picture.** Own guidance: next to each frame in the storyboard, one short line,
   "from: the pricing page, the third plan card" or "from: the changelog entry". A picture with no line is a
   picture that was invented.

## 4.2 The could-it-be-another-subject test
1. **If the prop could appear unchanged in another subject's video, it did not come from this source.**
   Replace it with something only this subject could produce, then decorate.
2. **A generic picture is a signal, not a sin.** Own guidance: some frames need a neutral background or a
   simple shape. The test is for the picture that is meant to carry meaning.
3. **Real material is the source's, not yours.** If the pictures come from a third party's site, product or
   people, who may use them is a question for `business/licence-compliance`, and for a person's likeness or
   data `business/data-protection`; the block states the question and does not answer it.

## 4.3 When the source is a live page
1. **A captured page is the source of truth for how it looks.** The product workflow uses the real screenshot
   as the base of a tour of a site rather than rebuilding the page, overlays real captured assets on it where
   something has to move, and rebuilds only the one component that moves. It recreates the whole page only if
   the person explicitly asks for a stylised version.
2. **An unusable capture does not license inventing the page.** See §5.1.
3. **Choose the frames' pictures from an inventory, not from browsing the raw files.** The workflow keeps one
   described list of captured assets and picks from it. Own guidance: write that list once, with what each
   asset is and where it came from, so the source lines in 4.1 have something to point to.
