# video-brief-and-storyboard §1 — The locked brief and the one-line strategy

> Part 1 of `business/video-brief-and-storyboard`. Read it when a video is starting or its brief changes.
> Sources, read 2026-10-20: the setup and storyboard steps of an Apache-2.0 product-video workflow, the router
> of the same framework, and the video skill of an MIT marketing skills repository (all read in clones). The
> brief and intent-interview reference files of that framework were not read. Sentences marked "own guidance"
> are ours. Not marketing advice (`business/README.md`).

## 1.1 Brief first
1. **Write the brief before anything is produced, and keep it in a file the work resumes from.** The workflow
   read treats the brief as the one document everything later reads: if it exists, the work reads it and asks
   nothing; if the project exists without one, it resumes from the files on disk rather than re-interviewing a
   half-built project; only a fresh request with neither goes through the interview. Own guidance: the same
   discipline works without any tool, as a short file at the top of the project.
2. **What the brief holds.** Own wording of what the two sources ask for before work starts:
   - the kind of video (explainer, launch, demo, tutorial, testimonial) and what it is for;
   - the audience, and the one message;
   - where it will be shown, which fixes the shape of the picture and its length, and is decided once at the
     start (landscape for a site or a long-form channel, portrait for a short-form feed, square for a feed
     post, as the marketing skills repository's own list has it; platforms change, so re-read the channel's
     page);
   - what source material exists (a page, a recording, a script, a data file);
   - whether it is a one-off or a template that will be run again with other data;
   - who approves the plan and who approves the first finished version.
3. **Say whether captions are in.** The marketing skills repository lists missing captions as a common mistake
   and says to add them on an explainer. Own guidance: write "captions: yes or no, and why" in the brief; the
   rules for captions themselves are `skills/accessibility` §7. The repository's statistic about how many
   people watch without sound has no source in the text and is not repeated here.
4. **Ask for what is missing, only that, and in one round.** Own guidance: a brief that is half-filled with
   guesses is worse than a short question, but a long questionnaire stalls the work. Ask once for the
   audience, the message, the channel and the source, and record the answers.

## 1.2 The one-line strategy
1. **State the strategy in one sentence: "this video tells [audience] that [message]."** The framework's story
   doctrine opens every plan with that line, echoed back to the person before the frames are shown (§3.1 here).
2. **The message is a claim a viewer could agree or disagree with.** "Our new release" is a topic, not a
   message; "teams that review in the browser stop losing comments between rounds" is a message. If the sentence
   cannot be argued with, the video has not been decided yet.
3. **One message per video.** Own guidance: a second message is a second video, or a second section the person
   has agreed to cut. A video with three messages gets remembered for none of them.
4. **Everything later is judged against the sentence.** The hook (§2), each frame's why (§3) and each picture
   (§4) have to trace back to it. A sentence that moves mid-way means the brief changes (1.3), not that the
   build quietly drifts.

## 1.3 Changing the brief
1. **A change of audience, message, channel or length is a change of brief.** Write it into the brief, tell the
   people who approved the plan, and redo the plan from the strategy line; do not patch frames to fit a new
   message.
2. **A recommendation keeps its reason.** The framework's doctrine says each recommendation (the shape of the
   story, the number of beats, any beat the person may question) states its basis. Own guidance: "because the
   message is X" is a basis; "because that is how videos are made" is not.

## 1.4 Approval points
1. **Two points need a person: the plan (§3) and the first finished preview.** The product workflow read puts
   its gates there: the storyboard is approved before any build, and the render waits for a final look. Own
   guidance: write down in the brief who gives each approval, so nobody discovers at the end that the person
   who could say yes was never asked.
2. **When no person is available, say that the plan was posted as a notice, not approved.** The workflow read
   does the same: it posts the summary and goes on. Do not describe that as sign-off.
