# image-alt-text-writing-pitfalls §2 — Writing the text

For informative and active images. The method is to describe the image's job on this page.

## 2.1 Context decides the wording
1. **Describe the purpose of the image, not everything visible.** The text is rarely a detailed
   description of the picture, unless the page is about that picture ("Using alt text properly").
2. **The same photo reads differently on different pages.** The "five mistakes" post uses one photo of a car
   lot for a business listing, an exposé, a real estate listing and a political campaign: each wants other
   words (mistake 2).
3. **Let the surrounding text carry the overall point**; the alt text supplies what the picture shows
   (same post).
4. **"Describe what you see" is not enough.** The post shows alt text of a screenshot that says an audit
   result exists but not what the result was. Ask what a person who cannot see it would miss (same post).

## 2.2 Phrasings to avoid
1. **Do not start with "image of", "photo of" or "picture of."** Assistive technology already announces
   the element as an image, and the repetition annoys listeners ("Using alt text properly"; "five mistakes",
   mistake 3).
2. **Do not add "logo" to a logo's text.** The company name is the useful part ("Using alt text
   properly").
3. **No line breaks inside the text.** They cause awkward announcements and pauses ("Using alt text
   properly").
4. **Write plain sentences with punctuation and correct spelling**, because screen readers read them aloud
   as sentences. There is no one formula for the length: be concise and complete about what matters
   ("five mistakes", mistake 4).

## 2.3 Keywords and captions
1. **Do not stuff keywords.** A keyword string is a bad listening experience; the post's example
   ("eating apple heart disease") is given as text that tells a listener nothing (introduction and mistake 4).
2. **Own guidance:** if a checklist asks for keywords in alt text, answer with the sentence a listener
   needs and let the page text carry the search terms.
3. **Do not copy the caption into the alt text.** A screen reader then says the same words twice. Captions
   add credit and detail; alt text gives the image's meaning in this context (mistake 5).
4. **Captions may be omitted, the attribute may not** (same post).
