# health-domain-caution-pitfalls §1 — What may be said

## 1.1 Inform, do not advise
1. **General information about a topic is not advice about a person.** Symptom: copy that says you probably have,
   you should take, or you do not need to see anyone. Check: does a sentence address a reader's own body with a
   conclusion or an instruction? Fix: describe the topic generally and say that a qualified professional can
   assess an individual case.
2. **Never name a diagnosis for a described symptom**, in copy, in a script or in an answer from a tool. Describing
   what a condition is, in general, is different from matching a person to it.
3. **Never tell a reader to start, change or stop a treatment**, including a supplement or a diet used for a
   condition.
4. **Keep the reader in charge of the decision**: the message gives context and a next step to a qualified person,
   not an outcome.

## 1.2 Urgent cases
1. **Write the urgent path first** where any reader may be in distress: what to do, who to call, in the local
   emergency number and language. The local number comes from the official source for the place, read and dated.
2. **Do not hide the path below the fold or behind a link** on pages that discuss serious symptoms or crisis topics.
3. **A tool that cannot judge urgency must say so and hand over**, not guess. Symptom: a calm answer to a message
   that described an emergency.
4. **Review the wording with a qualified person** for crisis and self-harm topics; follow their guidance on tone.

## 1.3 Disclaimers
1. **A disclaimer does not make a claim safe.** A page that says something unsupported and adds a footer is still
   saying it.
2. **State once, clearly, what the material is and is not**: general information, not a substitute for a
   professional. Put it where it is read, not in a collapsed footer.
3. **Match the disclaimer to the real reviewer.** Do not write reviewed by a professional unless someone with the
   title has reviewed it, and name them and the date.
4. **Do not claim credentials, affiliations or endorsements** the organisation does not hold.

## 1.4 Tone
1. **Avoid fear and shame as levers.** A message designed to alarm sells and harms.
2. **Avoid before-and-after framing** that implies a typical result; say it is not typical unless data shows it is,
   and cite the data.
3. **Respect different conditions of readers**: plain language, no jargon without explanation, accessible format.
