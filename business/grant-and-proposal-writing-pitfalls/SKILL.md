# grant-and-proposal-writing-pitfalls

Business layer (`business/README.md`). A proposal is scored by someone with a checklist and a stack of other
applications. It loses points for a requirement they could not find, not for prose that was merely plain. This block
is the check to run on a draft against the call. Choosing between vendors who answer a request of yours is
`business/vendor-management`; scoring claims a vendor makes is `skills/vendor-claims-and-scoring-pitfalls`; a pitch
to investors is `business/investor-relations`; a contract or its terms is `business/legal-documents`.

It states method only. Eligibility, funding rules, State-aid limits, intellectual-property clauses and anything
legally binding are read from the call text and decided by whoever signs. Not legal advice.

## When
- A call, tender or request for proposals has been published and a response is being planned.
- A draft exists and is about to be reviewed, costed or submitted.
- A rejected proposal is being debriefed before the next attempt.

## Steps

**Read only the section the task meets.** §1 at kickoff; §2 once a draft exists; §3 in the last week.

## Output / checkpoint
No pipeline checkpoint (business layer). What it owes: a requirements matrix with one row per requirement (where
answered, who owns it, status), a go or no-go note written before drafting, a budget reconciled line by line with
the narrative, a submission checklist ticked by a second person, and a debrief note after the result.

## Guardrails
- Build the matrix from the call text before drafting, and answer in the order and under the headings the call asks
  for. Never rely on memory of a previous call (§1).
- Decide go or no-go on eligibility, fit and capacity first. Never write a full response to a call you cannot
  legally or practically win (§1).
- Tie every budget line to a named activity and every activity to an outcome. Never leave a cost with no activity or
  an activity with no cost (§2).
- Say what will be measured, by whom and when. Never promise an outcome that has no measurement (§2).
- Respect limits as written (pages, characters, attachments). Never shrink fonts, margins or figures to fit (§1).
- Have a second person run the submission checklist and submit early. Never submit in the last hour (§3).
- Route eligibility, State-aid, intellectual-property and liability questions to the owner who signs. Never decide
  them in the draft (§1, §3).
- This block is a method from general practice, with submission points checked against two official portal pages;
  never run by us on a real call. Status and open points are in [`references/origin.md`](./references/origin.md).
