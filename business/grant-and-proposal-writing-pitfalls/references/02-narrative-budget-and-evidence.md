# grant-and-proposal-writing-pitfalls §2 — Narrative, budget and evidence

## 2.1 One story across sections
1. **State the problem, the approach, the outcome and the proof in the same words in the summary, the body and the
   budget justification.** Symptom: the summary promises one thing and the work plan delivers another. Check: read
   the summary, then the work plan, and list differences.
2. **Write the summary last, and have it read alone.** It is often the only part some reviewers read in full.
3. **Describe the need with a checked fact and its source**, not an adjective. An unsourced statistic is a claim a
   reviewer can discount. Mark any figure you have not re-read at its source, and date it.
4. **Say what happens without the funding.** A reviewer asks why this needs money now; answer it directly.

## 2.2 Budget and narrative reconcile
1. **Every cost line names the activity it pays for and the person-time or quantity behind it.** Symptom: a round
   total with no breakdown, or a role in the budget absent from the plan. Fix: a table with activity, quantity, unit
   cost, total, and the section where the activity is described.
2. **Check the arithmetic and the rules together**: totals, rates, caps on overheads, eligible and ineligible
   categories, co-funding share, currency and date of any exchange rate used. Read the rules in the call; do not
   reuse last year's.
3. **Costs and timeline agree**: an activity in month 18 has no salary in month 6, and the end date of the plan is
   the end date of the budget.
4. **Do not pad.** Reviewers compare with similar projects; unexplained margins lose trust faster than a modest
   budget.

## 2.3 Outcomes that can be checked
1. **Separate activities (what we do), outputs (what exists), outcomes (what changes).** Symptom: a list of tasks
   presented as impact.
2. **Give each outcome an indicator, a baseline, a target, an owner and a date.** If no baseline exists, say how it
   will be collected first.
3. **Do not state a target the evidence cannot support.** Re-read the evidence you cite and say what it does not
   show.
4. **Name risks with a response each**, including the likely one, not only generic ones.

## 2.4 Partners and roles
1. **One row per partner: what they do, what they receive, who signs.** Symptom: two partners both listed as lead
   on one task.
2. **Letters of support and commitment say what the call asks they say.** A warm letter without the required
   commitment may not count.
3. **Check that every partner is eligible in their own right** before they are named.
