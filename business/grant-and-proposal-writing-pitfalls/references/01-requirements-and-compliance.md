# grant-and-proposal-writing-pitfalls §1 — Requirements matrix and compliance

## 1.1 Build the matrix before the first paragraph
1. **List every obligation in the call**: each sentence with shall, must, will be required, or a number (limit,
   date, percentage). Symptom: a late discovery of a mandatory annex or a minimum partner count. Check: a second
   reader finds nothing in the call that is missing from the list. Fix: one row per obligation with section, owner
   and status.
2. **List the scoring criteria and their weights separately.** A requirement is pass or fail; a criterion earns
   points. Symptom: equal space given to a minor criterion and to the heaviest. Fix: size each section by its
   weight and put the most visible evidence where the criterion is scored.
3. **Mirror the structure and the vocabulary of the call.** A reviewer scoring criterion 3 looks for a heading that
   says so. Symptom: the answer exists but sits in a section called something else. Fix: headings follow the call,
   internal names do not.
4. **Mark every unknown as a question to the funder or owner**, with a date. Do not fill a gap with a guess; some
   funders publish answers to all applicants, so ask in the way the call allows.

## 1.2 Go or no-go first
1. **Check eligibility literally**: legal form, country, size, track record, co-funding, exclusions. Symptom: weeks
   of drafting, then an ineligible lead applicant. Fix: a signed-off yes from the owner of eligibility before
   drafting.
2. **Check capacity**: people, hours, partners available before the deadline, and what delivery would cost if won.
   A response nobody can deliver is a liability when it succeeds.
3. **Write the reason for go or no-go down.** A later debrief needs it.

## 1.3 Compliance review of a draft
1. **Run the matrix against the draft, not against memory**, row by row, by someone who did not write the section.
2. **Limits count as written**: pages, characters with or without spaces, attachments, file formats, language.
   Check what counts (references, tables, cover pages) in the call. Never shrink type or margins.
3. **Mandatory parts are checked for presence and for form**: signed, dated, on the funder's template, in the
   requested file type.
4. **Use the call's own terms for the same thing across all documents** (applicant, partner, work package). A name
   that changes between sections reads as two different projects.
5. **Do not leave template guidance text, comments or tracked changes** in the submitted file; search for them.
