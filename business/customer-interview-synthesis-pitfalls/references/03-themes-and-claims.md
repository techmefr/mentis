# customer-interview-synthesis-pitfalls §3 — Themes and claims

A theme feels solid because the quotes are vivid. The question is how many different people stand behind it, and what
the sample could not have shown.

## 3.1 Count people, not mentions
1. **Count distinct participants per theme.** Symptom: "the most common pain" is one person who said it eight
   times. Check: for each theme, list the participant codes; the count of codes is the strength.
2. **Report the count with the base**: three of seven, not "many". State how many people could have raised it.
3. **A line seen once is a lead, not a finding.** Mark it as something to probe in the next calls, not to build on.

## 3.2 A confidence label per finding
1. **Give each finding a label** (high, medium, low) from rules written before coding: how many distinct people,
   whether unprompted, whether it holds across the segments, whether behaviour agrees with words. The thresholds
   are the team's to set and write down.
2. **Write what would change the label**: another segment, a rival's customers, usage data.
3. **Say what the sample cannot show.** A small interview set suggests directions and surfaces problems; it does
   not measure how common a view is in a population. Do not turn "four of six" into a percentage of the market.

## 3.3 Segments and contradictions
1. **Split by segment before averaging.** A theme that appears in one segment and not in another is two
   findings. Symptom: a persona that fits nobody because it blends two groups.
2. **Keep the contradictions in the write-up**: people who said the opposite, or whose behaviour contradicts
   their words. A story with no counter-evidence was probably cleaned.
3. **Weight recent evidence more and say the date.** An old round may describe a different product and buyer.
4. **A source skews the picture**: reviews attract strong opinions, support tickets are problems, a survey
   reaches those who answer. Name the skew beside the finding.

## 3.4 From finding to action
1. **A persona is a summary of a segment, built from coded observations**, with each trait traceable to
   participants. A trait with no source is deleted, not guessed. Mark a persona built from proxy sources as
   provisional until first-hand evidence replaces it.
2. **A finding does not set a priority.** Cost, strategy and risk also enter; hand the finding to the product
   owner (`business/product-ownership`) with its confidence.
3. **A claim to the outside world needs more than interview quotes.** "Customers say" in a public page falls
   under `business/marketing-claim-substantiation-pitfalls`.
4. **When the stakes are high or the finding is surprising, test it another way**: usage data, a small
   experiment, more calls with the segment you lacked (`skills/assumption-validation-pitfalls`).
