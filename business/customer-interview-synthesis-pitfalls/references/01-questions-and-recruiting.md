# customer-interview-synthesis-pitfalls §1 — Questions and recruiting

An interview can only return what the question and the room allow. Most bad findings are made before the first
call: a guide that invites agreement, and a list of people chosen because they were easy to reach.

## 1.1 Start from the decision
1. **Write the decision the interviews feed** before the guide: what will change depending on what you hear.
   Symptom: ten pleasant calls and nothing to do with them. Fix: drop every question that cannot change the
   decision.
2. **Write what would prove you wrong.** Ask at least one question whose honest answer would be unwelcome.
   Check: if every question can only confirm the idea, rewrite the guide.

## 1.2 Past behaviour over opinion
1. **Ask about the last time**: what happened, what they did, what they tried before, what it cost. Symptom: a
   rich story with no event in it, only general statements ("we usually..."). Fix: pull them back to one
   concrete occasion.
2. **Avoid hypotheticals.** "Would you use...", "how much would you pay..." produce a polite guess. What people did,
   paid for or built around is evidence; what they predict about themselves is weak evidence.
3. **Avoid leading and closed questions**: a question that names the answer ("don't you find X slow?") or can be
   answered yes or no. Rewrite as an open question about the event.
4. **Do not pitch.** Describing the idea first turns the call into a review of the idea. If the idea must be
   shown, show it after the behaviour questions, and mark everything said afterwards as a reaction.
5. **Ask why the first answer is the answer.** Follow a stated reason down a few questions until you reach the
   situation behind it; the first answer is often a label for something else.

## 1.3 Compliments, promises and polite noise
1. **A compliment is not evidence of need.** "That sounds great" costs the speaker nothing. Note it and move on.
2. **A promise about the future is not a commitment.** "Send it when it's ready" is a soft no. A real signal costs
   the person something: time now, an introduction, access to data, money, a next meeting booked.
3. **Ask for the next step at the end of the call** and record what was agreed, including a refusal.

## 1.4 Who is in the room
1. **Write the segment you need and who you actually have.** Symptom: the interviewees are fans, or colleagues
   of a friendly account manager, or only users who answered a survey. Check: list how each was found.
2. **Include people who left, never bought, or use a rival.** A sample of satisfied customers explains why
   customers stay, nothing about why others do not.
3. **The user and the buyer are often different people.** Interview both and record which one spoke.
4. **Consent and recording.** Say before the call whether it is recorded and what happens to the notes; keep
   participants' details apart from the notes (`business/data-protection`).
5. **Keep calls casual enough to be honest**, without hiding the purpose: say you are gathering how people work,
   not selling. A rehearsed, formal call gets the polished answer.
