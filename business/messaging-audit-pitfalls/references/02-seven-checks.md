# messaging-audit-pitfalls §2 — The seven checks

Run all seven over the inventory of §1. They are the mechanics of the chrisrodde audit mode (MIT). The
practitioner frameworks that skill cites by name are books and methods; this block does not copy them.

## 2.1 Hero count and category count
1. **Hero count**: how many different things is the product called across the artefacts? List each with
   its source and date. More than one primary identity is the most common finding and the most damaging;
   most downstream inconsistencies trace back to it. Present it as a table; the length argues.
2. **Category count**: same exercise for the category. Watch for coined platform names: a coined category
   in an artefact suggests category creation was attempted at some point.
3. **Check whether that was decided or drifted in**, for example from an investor deck (same source).
4. **Own guidance:** one row per surface, with the exact phrase, so the table can be re-run next quarter.

## 2.2 Table stakes
1. **Take the top three to five differentiators the materials claim and search competitors' current
   marketing live.** Mark each: still differentiating, now table stakes, or never was.
2. **Search live, not from memory.** The skill's main file says competitor claims are checked with web
   search, never from memory.
3. **Keep a screenshot or a direct quote** from each competitor page; the specificity is what makes the
   finding land (chrisrodde).
4. **A claim sharp eighteen months ago may not be now**, especially when a large incumbent bundles the
   capability at no extra cost (same source). Own guidance: date each competitor check, because it is a
   snapshot.

## 2.3 Claim consistency and risk
1. **Look for the same claim at different strengths** across artefacts.
2. **Security and privacy claims first**: absolute statements ("never", "prohibited", "certified") in
   older collateral against carefully qualified wording in newer material. The source calls it a legal
   exposure, not just a consistency issue, and the highest-priority fix. Present these separately and
   first (§3.1). This block does not say what the law requires; hand them to the owner of legal and
   compliance (`business/data-protection`).
3. **Quantified outcomes**: the same metric attached to different subjects, for example "per engineer" in
   one place and "per product manager" in another.
4. **Credentials** such as years of experience, team background and certifications drift upward over time.
5. **Customer attribution**: named accounts in some materials and anonymised roles in others. Check
   whether a quote-clearance policy exists.

## 2.4 Sourcing
1. **Every statistic: does it have a source and a date? Are internal estimates labelled?**
2. **Unsourced numbers hurt in two directions**: a buyer who checks finds nothing, and a salesperson
   cannot defend the figure in the room.
3. **Where a number is unsourced, look for a credible published substitute** rather than just flagging
   the gap (chrisrodde). The full claim-by-claim procedure is `business/marketing-claim-substantiation-pitfalls`.

## 2.5 Vocabulary
1. **Build two columns**: terms customers use, and terms only the company uses.
2. **Source the first column from real customer material**: calls, transcripts, support tickets, reviews.
   If no customer says it, it is house language.
3. **House language is not worthless**: it often works internally. The usual recommendation is to keep it
   for internal alignment and retire it from customer-facing surfaces, not to delete it.

## 2.6 Specificity
1. **Run the competitor-swap test** on the homepage headline and the deck's opening slide: if a
   competitor's logo sits comfortably on top of the copy, the copy describes the category.
2. **Own guidance:** record the result as a quoted line plus a yes or no, so the check is repeatable.

## 2.7 Putting the checks in order
Own guidance, not from a source:
1. **Start with hero count and category count**: they explain most of what the other checks find.
2. **Do the security and privacy pass early**, because it produces the first-priority items.
3. **Table stakes needs web access**; where it is missing, say so and do not fill it from memory.
