# messaging-audit-pitfalls

Business layer (`business/README.md`), marketing. `business/product-marketing` §1 gives four positioning
sentences to write before any copy. This block is what to run **first when words already exist**: an audit
of what is out there, because a rewrite that does not name what went wrong reproduces it. Its premise:
inherited language is the main source of drift, and nobody reads the website, the decks and the one-pagers
side by side unless the audit makes someone do it.

Claim law and security or compliance wording rules were not verified here; absolute statements are surfaced
for the owner of legal and compliance, not judged by this block.

## When
- Before rewriting positioning, a messaging framework, a homepage or a deck.
- After a repositioning, a rename or a launch, to find what still tells the old story.
- Joining a team and needing to know what is already out there.
- The story feels inconsistent and nobody has read all of it together.

**Read only the section the task meets.** Run them in order for a full audit.

## Output / checkpoint
No pipeline checkpoint (business layer). What it owes: an inventory with dates, the stated reference
surface, evidenced mismatches quoting the exact wording and naming the surface, risk items first, the
strongest existing artefact named, a patch-or-rebuild recommendation with its reason, and the two or three
decisions that must be taken before writing. The decisions belong to whoever owns the message.

## Guardrails
- Never rewrite before the inventory has been read in full (§1.1, §1.2).
- Never report a mismatch you cannot show with quoted wording and the surface name (§1.4).
- Never fill the table-stakes check from memory; search the competitors' current pages (§2.2).
- Never bury an absolute security or privacy statement among cosmetic findings (§2.3).
- Never present a coined term as a customer's word without customer material behind it (§2.5).
- Never soften the structural finding, and never moralise about how the drift happened (§3.2).
- Never rewrite before the decisions of the audit are taken (§3.4).
- This block is a method from a young, unreviewed repository plus one small one, and was never run on a real
  company's material. A second human read is warranted. Source status and open points are in
