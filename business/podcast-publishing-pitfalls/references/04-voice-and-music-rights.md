# podcast-publishing-pitfalls §4 — Whose voice, whose music

> Part 4 of `business/podcast-publishing-pitfalls`. Read it when a guest, a synthetic or cloned voice, or music
> or stock sound is involved. METHOD ONLY, NOT LEGAL ADVICE: no statute is named, no obligation is stated, and
> this part was not checked against any platform's content policy or any licensing authority. Sources, read
> 2026-10-20: a speech skill repository (Apache-2.0) and the voice skills of a speech-vendor repository (MIT).
> A third repository without a licence file was not used (see origin). A person who owns the question decides
> (`business/README.md`).

## 4.1 A cloned voice needs a recorded yes
Own guidance, as questions to answer in writing before any clone is made:
1. **Whose voice is it, and did they say yes to this use?** A tool accepting a sample is not a yes. A public
   figure's published recordings are not a yes.
2. **What is the scope?** Which projects, which languages, for how long.
3. **How does the person withdraw, and what happens to episodes already published?**
4. **Where is the yes kept?** With the episode, next to the licence file (§4.3).

The vendor repository read ships a voice-conversion skill that suggests cloning a target voice from a short
sample and says nothing about consent. That is the gap this part exists for; the repository's text is not a
method and is not copied.

## 4.2 Say it is synthetic, where listeners meet it
1. The speech skill read tells the user to give end users a clear disclosure that a voice is AI-generated, and
   scopes custom voices out. Own guidance: write the notice in the episode intro or the notes, in true wording,
   as the listener will meet it, not in a settings page.
2. The words and the metadata for such notices are in `business/ai-content-disclosure-and-provenance-pitfalls`
   (§1 and §2 there); do not restate them here.
3. Personal-data and biometric questions about a voice go to `business/data-protection`.

## 4.3 Music and sound, with the licence on file
Own guidance, as questions; none is verified against a licensing source:
1. **Two rights can sit under one track**: the recording and the musical work. A licence says which use
   (podcast, video, ad), where, for how long, and whether paid promotion is covered.
2. **Stock libraries differ by tier.** "Royalty-free" describes how the fee is charged, not what is permitted.
3. **Keep the licence and the receipt with the episode**, the same habit as recording a dependency's licence at
   the point of use (`business/licence-compliance`).
4. **Generated music has the generating vendor's terms.** A vendor's refusal to take artist-named prompts is
   its policy, not a clearance of what it does produce.
