# ad-creative-testing-and-policy-pitfalls §1 — Creative as hypotheses

A creative set is easy to report as a number of ads and easy to test with a hypothesis that nothing can refute.
This section treats each creative idea as a claim about an audience that a result can contradict.

## 1.1 Inventory by concept, not by file
1. **List assets by what they are testing.** For each, record the concept (the idea about who and why), the
   hook, the offer, the format, the placement, the audience, the date it started and the window its results
   cover. One row per concept, with its executions underneath.
2. **A resize or a crop is not a new concept.** The same idea in another aspect ratio, a cropped cut or a
   re-exported file is the same concept in another placement. Count it as one concept with several
   executions, so that "twelve ads" that are three ideas is reported as three.
3. **Judge a concept across its executions.** If three executions of one idea all underperform, the idea is
   the suspect; if one of them does, the execution is (idea from the creative-strategy material of one
   source; own wording).
4. **Keep the inventory beside the evidence window.** A row with no date range cannot be compared with another
   row, and a concept that has run only in one placement says nothing about the others.

## 1.2 A hypothesis that can lose
1. **Each test carries a falsifiable statement.** Name the metric, the comparison (against which control or
   which other concept, in which audience) and the expected direction. "Beats the control on click rate among
   existing customers" can lose; "this will resonate" cannot.
2. **Add a size only where there is a baseline.** An expected magnitude is honest when a prior figure exists to
   anchor it. Without one, say the direction and leave the number empty rather than invent it.
3. **Let the hypothesis follow from the idea.** If the concept says buyers are held back by one worry, the
   test statement says what should change if that is true, so a loss teaches something about the audience and
   not only about an asset.
4. **Change one thing per test.** A new hook with a new offer and a new format cannot say which of the three
   moved the result. The design of the test itself (assignment, sample, stopping) is
   `skills/experiment-design-pitfalls`; this section does not restate it.
5. **Do not carry a promise of compounding gains.** A claim such as "several simultaneous tests each add a
   little, so the funnel improves by a stated percentage a quarter" has no source and is not carried.

## 1.3 Fatigue is read from a time series
1. **Fatigue is a change in delivery and response over time, not an age.** An ad being old, or having run a
   number of weeks, is not evidence. Look at the series: response per unit of delivery by day, cost per
   result, and how many times the same people were reached, for this ad against its own earlier level.
2. **State the comparison before reading.** Against the ad's own first stable days, or against a sibling ad
   running at the same time. A decline that appears in every ad at once points at the audience, the season or
   the tracking, not at one creative's wear (`business/ad-measurement-pitfalls` §3, `skills/metric-movement-diagnosis-pitfalls`).
3. **Check lag and the change history before blaming the creative.** Recent days are incomplete and a
   configuration change in the window moves every series
   (`business/paid-media-decision-pitfalls` §2).
4. **Carry no universal refresh cadence and no frequency or drop band.** The sources disagree with each other
   on both, and one gives lifespans per format while another states there is no universal cadence. Write the
   account's own trigger and the history it was checked against, as for a kill rule
   (`business/paid-media-decision-pitfalls` §3.3).
5. **Keep a replacement ready before retiring a producer**, and judge the replacement on its own window, not on
   its first days (`business/paid-media-decision-pitfalls` §3.3).

## 1.4 Three separate judgements
1. **Creative quality, format fit and measured performance are three findings.** Whether the idea is clear and
   the offer evidenced is one question; whether the asset fits the placement it runs in is another; whether it
   performed is a third. A high-performing ad with a placement problem and a clear ad that has no data yet are
   different findings and are written as such.
2. **Format fit is checked against the current page of the platform in use.** This block carries no size,
   length, duration or safe-zone figure, because they change; open the active platform's current creative
   specification and record its address and date (`re-read before relying`).
3. **Emotional and cultural judgement stays with a person.** A test can say which of two ads people clicked;
   it does not say whether one of them is tasteless or misreads a culture.
4. **A claim in the copy is a separate question.** Whether "cuts reporting time" is true and can be
   substantiated belongs to `business/marketing-claim-substantiation-pitfalls`; creative review here asks only
   that someone has run it.
