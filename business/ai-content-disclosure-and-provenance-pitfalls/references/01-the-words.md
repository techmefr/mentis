# ai-content-disclosure-and-provenance-pitfalls §1 — The words

## 1.1 Say what the AI did
1. **A bare "Made with AI" label does not say whether the asset was fully generated or partly edited**, and
   it can erode trust when the creator only lightly touched small details (C2PA user-experience
   recommendations, 3.3.2 Source Disclosures, in the 2.2 edition).
2. **Describe the core process objectively:** generated by an AI tool, made without generative AI, or taken
   from a camera (same section). Name the part when it is known. Own guidance: "background generated, subject
   photographed" says more than either extreme.
3. **Avoid value judgements** about whether the AI changed the meaning of the content (same section).
4. **Claims such as "AI-edited" or "AI-enhanced" should come with verifiable context about how the AI
   affected the content**; the standard discourages showing them without it (same section). If you cannot
   supply the context, leave the claim out or say what you can support.
5. **When several assets are combined, say which one the statement concerns** (same section, on multiple
   manifests).

## 1.2 Say only what is true
1. **A disclosure sentence claims only what happened.** The design note's default text speaks of review by
   an editorial team because its pipeline always performs a review step and a human approval (indranilbanerjee
   contentforge, design note of 2026-08-13, component A, invariants). If no review took place, the sentence
   may not say so.
2. **When an author is named, the sentence says what that person did** (researched, checked, edited), and
   the wording works cleanly when no author is named (same note).
3. **Keep the default wording vendor-neutral**, with no model or company names; a brand may name them in its
   own custom text (same note).
4. **Own guidance:** keep a record of who reviewed what and when, so the sentence can be audited later.

## 1.3 When unsure, disclose
1. **Over-disclosure is harmless; under-disclosure is the risk.** The design note makes an uncertain
   situation default to showing the disclosure (component A, surface gate).
2. **Own guidance:** record the reason whenever a disclosure is left off, with the person who decided.
3. **Where the law or a platform requires disclosure, ask the owner of legal and compliance** and cite the
   official text. Dates and applicability were not verified here.
4. **Show it where people see it.** The standard suggests a short statement at the first level of display
   with more detail one step further in, because laws may require direct disclosure (3.3.2).
