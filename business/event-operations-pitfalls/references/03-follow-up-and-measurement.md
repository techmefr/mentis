# event-operations-pitfalls §3 — Follow-up and measurement

## 3.1 Capture and follow up
1. **Record, after each conversation, what the person said, what they care about and the agreed next step.**
   Symptom: a spreadsheet of names with no context and a generic message. Fix: a note per contact, written the same
   day.
2. **Follow up within days, referring to what was discussed.** The events skill sets the window at one to two days while
   the conversation is fresh; treat that and any claim that delay lowers replies as its author's view unless you have your
   own data.
3. **Tier the follow-up**: a conversation with a target gets a personal note and a concrete next step; a warm
   contact gets material tied to their stated problem; a scan with no conversation gets one light message or none.
4. **Respect consent and opt-out**: send marketing only where the registration said so
   (`business/email-deliverability-and-sender-requirements-pitfalls`, `business/outbound-sales-and-crm-hygiene-pitfalls`).
5. **Route each contact into the CRM with the event as its source**, one owner per contact.

## 3.2 Measure
1. **Count useful conversations with target accounts, meetings held and deals influenced**, not scans or
   attendance. Symptom: a report headed by the number of badges.
2. **Compute cost per useful conversation** from all costs in §2.4, and say how many events the figure rests on.
3. **Allow for a long cycle**: results may arrive months later; set review dates and name the leading signals.
4. **Name what is not measured**, such as brand effect or conversations not logged.
5. **Do not claim an event caused a deal** that other touches influenced; say influenced, and show the other
   touches.
6. **Tag the source and ask the attendee.** The events skill recommends source tagging plus self-reported attribution and
   says not to give last-click credit to an event-touched deal; record how a contact says they heard of you beside what the
   tool says (`business/b2b-go-to-market-pitfalls` §3.1.4).
7. **Separate vanity from real measures.** It groups registrations and scans as vanity, qualified conversations, meetings
   and pipeline as real, and cost per qualified meeting as the decisive figure, and lists badge scans as not leads.

## 3.3 Debrief and reuse
1. **Hold a short debrief within two weeks** with everyone who worked the event: what worked, what did not, what to
   change, repeat or drop.
2. **Update the target list, the no-list and the budget view.**
3. **Reuse the material**: a talk can become an article, a recording, a short clip, with review for claims and
   consent (`business/content-creation`).
4. **Thank speakers, partners and organisers**, and ask what they saw; they often know what you missed.
