# internal-comms-async-pitfalls §4 — Meetings

GitHub's guide treats a live meeting as a point of escalation by complexity, not the opening move. The
rules below say how to call one that earns its time.

## 4.1 Before
1. **Escalate to a meeting when the topic calls for it**: interpersonal conflict or connection, problem
   solving, or when written discussion has not reached a resolution (GitHub).
2. **Give it a goal and an agenda, unless it is informal or social.** Phrase agenda items as questions so it
   is easy to tell whether the goal was met; GitHub's guide says people may decline a meeting without
   an agenda. A running notes document works for recurring meetings (GitHub).
3. **Share the agenda ahead of time** to collect input from people who cannot attend live, so their views
   are heard in the call (GitHub's leaders' tips).
4. **Send information as a pre-read.** Use the meeting to discuss information, not to convey it (GitHub).
5. **Announce in writing before the meeting, never first in it**, so everyone has access, even those who
   cannot attend, and time to form questions (GitHub).
6. **Invite the fewest right people**, and let them leave if it is not a good use of their time (GitHub).
7. **Do not use a meeting** for status updates, to force work to happen (for example booking time with
   someone so they finish a task), or for anything better handled in writing (GitHub).

## 4.2 During
1. **Start and end on time**; if you finish early, use the time to talk informally (GitHub). The Clef
   handbook (CC0) puts the same responsibility on the leader and the attendees.
2. **Leave gaps between meetings.** GitHub suggests ending meetings 5 or 10 minutes early, or starting that much late, so people can be human between calls.
3. **Choices from one company, as examples**: Clef asks for availability for face-to-face meetings in a
   fixed window (10:30 to 13:00 Pacific, with 24 hours' notice) and a video option for remote people;
   GitHub schedules no-meeting days.
4. **Give several ways to take part** (hand raising, chat); in a large meeting consider a separate chat
   channel, because screen readers read the meeting chat over the speaker (GitHub).

## 4.3 After
1. **Memorialise the decisions** in a durable medium; an ad hoc meeting does not necessarily settle a
   technical question (GitHub; §2.1).
2. **Post pass-down notes** after the call (GitHub's leaders' tips).
3. **Record the meeting** unless it is personal or sensitive, so those who could not attend can catch up;
   a transcript makes details easier to find (GitHub).
4. **One-way content goes to a recording**, not a live slot: readouts, demos and announcements that need no
   interaction (GitHub, §1.1).
