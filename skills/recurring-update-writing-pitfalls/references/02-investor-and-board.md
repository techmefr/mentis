# recurring-update-writing-pitfalls §2 — Investor and board updates

An update to people who gave money or oversight has one job: let them check what you said before and see
what changed. The structures here are examples from open-source skills; none is a rule of law.

## 2.1 Numbers and bad news
1. **Numbers over adjectives.** Pitchdrive's example: a figure with its amount beats "an amazing month".
2. **Open with the single most important fact**, whether a number, a launch or a miss (Pitchdrive).
3. **Put bad news in the first sentence of its section**, never buried (Pitchdrive). The board-deck-builder
   skill agrees: never bury it, boards find out anyway, and late is worse.
4. **A bad month is stated plainly with the reason**, then the response. The board-deck-builder offers a
   four-step order: state it, own the cause, show you understand it, present the dated fix.
5. **Name the system and date each figure came from**, in a small footnote (Pitchdrive).
6. **"Nothing significant this month" is acceptable.** Pitchdrive prefers a short, honest update to padding.

## 2.2 Commitments revisited
1. **Keep a log of what you promised and asked** in each update, with the date (Pitchdrive keeps one file for
   this).
2. **In the next update, revisit each commitment explicitly**: done, partial or missed, one line each. Never
   drop one silently (Pitchdrive).
3. **A board deck can use the same shape**: where we said we would be, where we are, why the gap, what we do
   about it (board-deck-builder). One cause per variance, not a list of excuses.

## 2.3 Forecast, actuals and money
1. **Show actuals and projections separately**, clearly labelled (Pitchdrive).
2. **Give a forecast with a confidence level**, for example a main case and an upside with its condition
   (board-deck-builder).
3. **Keep signed or received money apart from verbal commitments**, on separate lines, and never blend them
   (ir-kit's update prompt).
4. **One sentence of cause per variance** to plan, and a table with the previous period and the target for
   each metric (board-deck-builder, an example of six to eight metrics).
5. **A short executive summary** of three sentences in that skill's example: state of the business, the
   biggest event, what comes next.

## 2.4 Asks and housekeeping
1. **Make asks specific and answerable**: a named kind of introduction or role, not "any help appreciated"
   (Pitchdrive, board-deck-builder).
2. **End with a confidentiality line** (Pitchdrive's example wording asks readers not to distribute).
3. **An unlisted link is not private** (Pitchdrive): anyone who receives or is forwarded it can read the
   figures. Decide the access mode before sharing and say so out loud; own guidance: attach a file when in
   doubt.
4. **Show the draft to its owner and wait for approval before anything is sent** (Pitchdrive).
5. **Separate versions for different audiences are a disclosure choice**, not a writing rule. ir-kit keeps a
   shorter prospect version without cash or runway; that is one author's decision and is not carried. See the
   guardrail on disclosure in the SKILL.
