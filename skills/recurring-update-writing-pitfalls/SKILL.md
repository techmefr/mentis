# recurring-update-writing-pitfalls

Not a step of the pipeline: a craft block for updates that come back every week, month or quarter. The
premise: **a recurring update earns trust by being checkable, and loses it the first time a number was
invented, a bad item was buried or a promise quietly vanished**. For where a team's messages and decisions
live see `internal-comms-async-pitfalls`; for the record of an incident see `blameless-incident-review-pitfalls`.

## When
- Writing or reviewing a weekly team update, a company newsletter or a short FAQ.
- Drafting an investor update or a board deck from figures someone else supplied.
- An update says "good progress" with no number, or last month's asks have disappeared.
- Preparing the documents an outside party will cross-check against each other.

## Output / checkpoint
Check a draft by reading only its first sentence of each section: the most important fact, including any
bad news, must be in it. Then take every number and ask where, from which system and on what date it came; a
number with no answer is removed or marked missing. For an update that is part of a series, list last
period's commitments and find each one's status (done, partial or missed). Nothing here was run with a
company's real figures.

## Guardrails
- Never invent a figure, a customer, a quote or a milestone; say it is missing (§3.1).
- Never bury bad news below good news (§2.1).
- Never drop a past commitment silently; state its status (§2.2).
- Never mix a forecast or a verbal commitment into actuals (§2.3).
- Never fill a missing document with a placeholder that looks real (§3.3).
- Sections, word counts, review times and the cadence of updates here are single-source examples (§1, §2);
  this block gives no securities, disclosure, tax or legal rule, and what may be shared with whom must be
  checked against the official texts and a qualified person.
