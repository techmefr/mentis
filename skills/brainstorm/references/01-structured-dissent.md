# brainstorm §1 — Structured dissent

> Section 1 of `skills/brainstorm`. Read it when a decision has several credible paths and no obvious winner:
> monorepo or several repositories, ship now or hold for polish, flag or full rollout, narrow the scope or keep the
> breadth. Not for verifying whether something is correct (`skills/gate`), breaking work into steps (`skills/plan`)
> or designing a structure (`skills/archi`).

1. **Reduce the decision to one question.** What are we deciding, which constraints are fixed, what counts as
   success. If you cannot write it in three lines, the discussion is not ready; ask one clarifying question first.
2. **Write your own position before hearing anyone.** Your preferred option, the three strongest reasons, the
   main risk of it. Written first so that the synthesis does not simply mirror whichever voice spoke last.
3. **Take three further voices, each with one lens**:
   - the skeptic challenges the premise and the assumptions and asks whether a simpler framing exists;
   - the pragmatist asks about shipping speed, user impact and what operating it really costs;
   - the critic looks for edge cases, downside risk and the ways it fails.
4. **Give each voice only the question and the minimum context**, in a fresh context, not the running conversation.
   That is the whole mechanism against anchoring: a voice that has read your reasoning will agree with your
   reasoning. Run them independently of each other too.
5. **Each answer is short and committed**: a position, the three best reasons, the main risk, and what would
   change its mind.
6. **Synthesize with the disagreement left visible.** Report where the voices agree, where they split and why,
   which concern survived the others, and your recommendation; name the strongest dissent even if you overrule it.
   If you changed your mind, say what changed it.
7. **The operator decides.** Dissent informs a choice; it does not take one. Record the outcome with its reason
   (`skills/documentation-adr` when the decision is structural).
8. **Do not convene it for a decision that has a deterministic answer**, a plain factual question or an obvious
   execution task. Four voices on a question one command settles is theatre.
