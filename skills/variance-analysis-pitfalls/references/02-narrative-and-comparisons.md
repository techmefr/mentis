# variance-analysis-pitfalls §2 — Narrative and comparisons

> Section 2 of `skills/variance-analysis-pitfalls`. Read it when commentary is written or a forecast is judged.
> Sourced from the same variance-analysis skill (commit `33eeda1`), sections on narrative generation and on
> budget versus actual versus forecast comparisons, read 2026-10-16. Sentences marked "own guidance" are ours.

## What a good explanation contains
1. **Name the driver.** Not "higher than expected", but the actual driver.
2. **Quantify each driver,** in amount and in share of the variance, so the sentences can be checked against
   the bridge of part 1.
3. **Give the cause,** why it happened and not only what happened.
4. **Say where it goes next** (one-off, continuing, improving, deteriorating) and **what follows** (nothing,
   monitor, investigate, update the forecast).
5. **Be short.** The source asks for two to four sentences per variance. Own guidance: a longer text usually
   hides a driver nobody could name.

## Anti-patterns
6. **Circular:** revenue was higher because revenue was higher.
7. **Vague:** "expenses were elevated" with no line or reason.
8. **Bare "timing":** it must say what was early or late and when it will normalise.
9. **Bare "one-time":** it must say what the item was.
10. **"Various small items"** for a material variance: decompose further.
11. **Only the biggest driver:** offsetting items are part of the explanation.

## Which comparison for which purpose
12. **Actual against budget** serves annual measurement and board reporting, because the budget is set once.
    **Actual against forecast** serves operations, because the forecast is refreshed. **Forecast against
    budget** shows how expectations moved since planning. **Actual against prior period or prior year** serves
    trends, seasonality and cases where a budget means little (a new line, a recent acquisition). Own guidance:
    say in the heading which one the commentary uses; a mix of bases in one paragraph is how a favourable and
    an unfavourable story both appear true.

## Forecast accuracy and bias
13. **Track accuracy over time.** The source's measure is one minus the absolute gap divided by the actual,
    and the mean of the absolute percentage gaps across periods. Own guidance: both divide by the actual, so
    define what happens when an actual is zero before using them.
14. **Read the pattern of variances, not only each one.** The source reads consistently favourable variances as
    a possibly padded plan, consistently unfavourable ones as a plan too aggressive or an execution problem,
    a growing unfavourable one as deterioration or an unrealistic target, a shrinking one as improving
    accuracy through the year, and a volatile one as an unpredictable business or a weak method. Own guidance:
    these are questions to put to the owner of the plan, not conclusions.
15. **Thresholds are not carried.** The source's percentage table and its materiality range were not sourced
    and are dropped; take the line from your organisation, and keep it the same across periods.
