# kafka-delivery-semantics-pitfalls §1 — Delivery semantics: offsets, retries and external sinks

Sources: the Apache Kafka documentation, "Design" page of the 4.3 documentation (apache/kafka-site at e6cbeb5, `content/en/43/design/design.md`: sections "The Consumer", "Message Delivery Semantics"), read 2026-10-15. Version marks below are the ones the page states.

## 1.1 The three guarantees
1. **The page defines them like this:** at most once means messages may be lost and are never redelivered; at least once means they are never lost and may be redelivered; exactly once means each message is processed once and only once. It adds that the problem splits in two: the durability of publishing and the guarantee when consuming.
2. **Read "exactly once" claims carefully.** The page warns that many systems claim it and that the claim is often misleading: it may not hold when consumers or producers fail, with several consumer processes, or when data written to disk can be lost.
3. **Kafka's default is at least once.** The page says at-most-once is something the user can implement by disabling producer retries and committing offsets before processing a batch.

## 1.2 Where the consumer saves its position
1. **Position saved before the processing gives at most once.** If the consumer crashes after saving its position and before saving its output, the process that takes over starts after messages that were never processed.
2. **Position saved after the processing gives at least once.** If the consumer crashes after processing and before saving the position, the first messages the replacement receives were already processed.
3. **Make the sink idempotent when you choose at least once.** The page's example: when messages have a primary key, writing the same message twice just overwrites a record with a copy of itself.
4. **The position is a single integer per partition,** the offset of the next message to consume, and a consumer can deliberately rewind it to re-consume. The page gives the case of a bug found after messages were consumed.

## 1.3 The producer side
1. **A producer that gets a network error cannot know whether the message was committed,** before or after the failure. The page compares it to inserting into a table with an autogenerated key.
2. **Resending without the idempotent option can write the message twice.** Before 0.11.0.0 resending was the only choice, which is at-least-once.
3. **Since 0.11.0.0 the producer has an idempotent delivery option:** the broker assigns each producer an ID and deduplicates with a sequence number sent with every message, so a resend does not duplicate entries in the log.
4. **The producer chooses its durability level:** wait for the commit (the page says on the order of 10 ms), send completely asynchronously, or wait only for the leader. The settings and their cost are in §2.

## 1.4 Writing to an external system
1. **The difficulty is to keep the consumer position and the output in agreement.** The classic answer is a two-phase commit between the two stores, which many output systems do not support.
2. **The simpler and more general answer in the page is to store the offset in the same place as the output,** so that data and offset are both updated or neither is. Its example is a connector that writes data to HDFS together with the offsets it read.
3. **For systems where messages have no primary key to deduplicate on, the page says Kafka follows similar patterns** that store offsets alongside the data. When a key exists, idempotent writes (§1.2.3) are the other route.
4. **Exactly-once delivery to other destination systems generally needs the cooperation of that system.** Kafka provides primitives that make it feasible, not the guarantee itself.

## 1.5 Checks (own guidance)
1. Name where the offset is committed relative to the write to the sink; if it is before, the pipeline is at most once.
2. Kill the consumer between the write and the offset commit in a test and show that the replay leaves the sink unchanged.
3. Name the key the sink deduplicates on, or the place where the offset is stored with the data.
