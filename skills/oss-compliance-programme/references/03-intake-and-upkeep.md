# oss-compliance-programme §3 — Intake and upkeep

> Section 3 of `skills/oss-compliance-programme`. Read it when the programme must handle outside questions,
> contributions, training or its own review. Source: `Example-Generic-Company-Policy-Text.md` of the
> OpenChain Reference-Material repository (commit `e79a0b7`), re-read 2026-10-16. Intervals and periods in
> square brackets in the source are placeholders and are not carried. Sentences marked "own guidance" are ours.

1. **Give outside questions one door.** Anyone who receives a compliance question from outside the company
   passes it to the named outside contact, who works with the lead and may hand parts to colleagues or to
   counsel. Publish the contact where outsiders can find it.
2. **Handle a reported issue in six moves.** Acknowledge receipt and say when you will answer; decide whether
   there is a real issue and, if not, say so to the sender; if there is, rank it against written severity
   criteria; choose the response against written response criteria; carry it out, changing contract terms or
   releases where needed; record everything in the decision log. The severity and response criteria are yours to
   write; the source leaves them to an appendix we do not reproduce.
3. **Check what a project asks of contributors before contributing.** The source says that contributing to
   another project may require a contributor agreement or a certificate of origin, that a blanket agreement may
   already exist, and that the lead checks before anyone contributes and clears the use of a company address.
   Two policy options are offered (encourage, or only with approval); choose one and write it.
4. **Say what staff may do outside work.** The source reminds staff of their duties on trade secrets,
   confidentiality and the company's intellectual property, and asks them to clear involvement that may be
   connected with their work. What applies to your staff depends on contracts and local law, which is for counsel
   (own guidance).
5. **Train by role and keep the records.** The source asks that everyone in the programme is trained on the
   skills their role needs, at least at a basic level, and that assessments are recorded. Which roles need
   what, and how long records are kept, are the owner's and HR's decisions.
6. **Record that people have read the policy.** A click-through or a signature, kept. The source's last clause
   (the policy is not part of employment terms and may change) is employment law drafting that we do not carry.
7. **Review the whole programme on a fixed date.** The source reviews the policy's effectiveness together
   with the affirmation of conformance, at an interval of at least the length it leaves in brackets. Set your
   own interval and write the next date in the policy.
8. **Claim conformance only after checking the standard.** The source talks of conformance to a named
   international standard and of public declarations. That standard was not read for this block, so this block
   makes no claim that following it satisfies the standard. If you want to claim conformance, read the
   standard's own text and, where a certification body or the project's own self-certification process
   applies, use it.
9. **Keep a short list of what changed.** When a release process, a delivery mode or the scope changes, record
   the change and whether the policy needs updating (own guidance).
