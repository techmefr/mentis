# nis2-incident-reporting-and-measures-pitfalls §1 — Scope and transposition

> Part 1 of `skills/nis2-incident-reporting-and-measures-pitfalls`. Read it before a duty is stated or planned.
> Source: Directive (EU) 2022/2555, Articles 2, 3, 4 and 41, Official Journal of 27.12.2022,
> https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32022L2555 , read 2026-10-19. Original
> publication, not a consolidated version; amendments were not checked. Statements are in our own words. Sentences
> marked "own guidance" are ours.

1. **The directive applies to types of entity listed in two annexes (Article 2(1)).** It covers public or private
   entities of a type in Annex I or II that are at least medium-sized under the Commission recommendation on
   enterprise size and that provide services or carry out activities in the Union. The annexes were not read: which
   sector and type the organisation falls under is a question for counsel.
2. **Some entities are covered whatever their size (Article 2(2) to (4)).** Examples read: providers of public
   electronic communications networks or services, trust service providers, TLD registries and DNS providers, sole
   providers of an essential service in a member state, some public administration entities, entities identified as
   critical under another directive, and domain name registration services. Own guidance: size is therefore not a safe
   reason to stop the analysis.
3. **Two categories (Article 3).** Entities of an Annex I type above the medium-size ceilings are essential
   entities, as are several named categories (for example qualified trust service providers, TLD registries, DNS
   providers); remaining entities of an annex type are important entities. Member states may also identify entities.
   Articles 20, 21 and 23 as read speak of both categories together; any difference in supervision was not read.
4. **Member states keep lists and ask entities to register (Article 3(3) and (4)).** States must establish a list
   of essential and important entities, and require entities to submit details such as name, contact data and sector,
   and to notify changes without delay and in any event within two weeks. Own guidance: find out whether the
   organisation must register in each member state where it operates, and who owns that registration.
5. **Other sector acts can replace parts of the directive (Article 4(1)).** Where a sector-specific Union act
   imposes measures or incident notification at least equivalent in effect, the relevant provisions of this directive
   do not apply to those entities. Which act, if any, applies to us, and whether it is equivalent, is for counsel; do
   not assume either way.
6. **National transposition is the operative layer (Article 41).** The directive is addressed to member states;
   they had to adopt and publish measures to comply and to apply them from 18 October 2024, with a reference to the
   directive. Own guidance: for every member state in which the organisation provides services, locate the national
   law and its competent authority and CSIRT, read it, and record the date read. Do not state a national rule, period,
   threshold or penalty until you have read that text; this block read none.
7. **Question list for counsel (own guidance).** Which entity in the group is covered, and as essential or important;
   in which member states; whether another sector act displaces the directive for us; which national law, authority
   and channel apply in each state; whether registration is due; whether suppliers or customers are caught indirectly.
   Record the written answer with date and name.
