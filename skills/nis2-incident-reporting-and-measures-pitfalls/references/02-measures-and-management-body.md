# nis2-incident-reporting-and-measures-pitfalls §2 — Measures and management body

> Part 2 of `skills/nis2-incident-reporting-and-measures-pitfalls`. Read it when measures are inventoried, a gap
> list is built or management is briefed. Source: Directive (EU) 2022/2555, Articles 20 and 21, read 2026-10-19
> (see part 1 for the edition). Conditional on counsel confirming scope (part 1). Own words; "own guidance" marks
> ours.

1. **Management approves and oversees (Article 20(1)).** Member states must ensure that the management bodies of
   essential and important entities approve the Article 21 measures, oversee their implementation, and can be held
   liable for infringements of that article, without prejudice to national liability rules for public institutions.
   Own guidance: record who approved what and when; an approval nobody can date is no approval. What liability means
   in practice depends on national law (part 1, point 6).
2. **Management training (Article 20(2)).** Members of the management bodies are to be required to follow training,
   and entities encouraged to offer similar training to staff, so that they can identify risks and assess practices.
   Own guidance: keep an attendance record, and leave the format to counsel and the national text.
3. **The measures must be proportionate and risk-based (Article 21(1) and (2)).** Entities take appropriate and
   proportionate technical, operational and organisational measures to manage risks to the network and information
   systems they use, and to prevent or minimise the impact of incidents. Proportionality takes account of exposure,
   size, likelihood and severity of incidents. The approach is all-hazards, and includes the physical environment.
4. **The listed minimum, as an inventory (Article 21(2)(a) to (j)).** The measures include at least: risk analysis
   and security policies; incident handling; business continuity (backups, recovery, crisis management); supply
   chain security, including direct suppliers; security in acquisition, development and maintenance, including
   vulnerability handling and disclosure; assessing the effectiveness of the measures; basic cyber hygiene and
   training; cryptography and encryption policies; human resources security, access control and asset management;
   multi-factor or continuous authentication, secured communications and emergency communication where appropriate.
5. **Turn each item into a row with evidence (own guidance).** For each of the ten items: the owner, the document or
   configuration that shows it, the date last reviewed, and the known gap. An item with a policy but no evidence of
   operation is a gap, not a pass. Engineering evidence typically lives in asset inventory, access reviews, backup
   restore tests, dependency and patch records, and incident exercises.
6. **Suppliers are weighed individually (Article 21(3)).** When choosing supply chain measures, entities consider the
   vulnerabilities specific to each direct supplier, the overall quality of their products and cybersecurity
   practices, including secure development, and the results of coordinated risk assessments referred to in the
   directive. Own guidance: a supplier register with what each supplier can reach and what was asked of it. Contract
   wording is for counsel.
7. **Non-compliance must be corrected (Article 21(4)).** An entity that finds it does not comply takes, without undue
   delay, the necessary, appropriate and proportionate corrective measures. Own guidance: a known gap with no dated
   remediation plan is worse than an unknown one; keep the plan with an owner.
8. **More detail may exist elsewhere (Article 21(5)).** The Commission was to adopt implementing acts on technical and
   methodological requirements for certain entity types. They were not read; check whether one applies, and which
   standards counsel or the national authority point to.
