# ai-act-obligations-and-omnibus-status-pitfalls §1 — Status and dates

> Part 1 of `skills/ai-act-obligations-and-omnibus-status-pitfalls`. Read it before any date or duty is stated.
> Sources, all read 2026-10-19: Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 Article 113 as originally published
> (https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32024R1689); Commission proposal COM(2025) 836 final
> of 19.11.2025 (CELEX:52025PC0836); the Parliament Legislative Observatory file 2025/0359(COD); Regulation (EU)
> 2026/1744 of 8 July 2026, Article 1 points 5, 7, 8, 39 and 40 and Article 4 (CELEX:32026R1744). Statements are in
> our own words. Every date below is "re-read before relying". Sentences marked "own guidance" are ours.

1. **The original text staged the dates (Article 113 as published).** It applied from 2 August 2026 as a general rule,
   with exceptions: Chapters I and II from 2 February 2025; Chapter III Section 4, Chapter V, Chapter VII, Chapter XII
   and Article 78 from 2 August 2025 (Article 101 excepted); Article 6(1) and its obligations from 2 August 2027.
   Own guidance: that is the starting text, no longer the whole answer, see points 3 and 4.
2. **The "Digital Omnibus on AI" began as a proposal.** COM(2025) 836 of 19.11.2025 proposed, among other things, to link
   the high-risk rules to the availability of standards or support tools, to turn the AI literacy duty into an
   encouragement, and to drop the registration of systems a provider has concluded are not high-risk under
   Article 6(3). A proposal binds nobody. Own guidance: a news item or a vendor deck that says "the AI Act is delayed"
   is evidence that something was proposed, not that it applies.
3. **It was adopted.** The Legislative Observatory file shows the procedure as completed: Parliament decision at first
   reading on 16.06.2026, Council adoption on 29.06.2026, signature on 08.07.2026, publication in the Official
   Journal on 24.07.2026, as Regulation (EU) 2026/1744. Its Article 4 provides entry into force on the third day
   after publication. Own guidance: the adopted act is not the proposal; several dates in the proposal differ from
   the act, so read the act.
4. **What the act changes on dates (Article 1 point 40, amending Article 113).** As read: Article 5(1) points (ba) and
   (bb) and Article 5(1a) and (1b) apply from 2 December 2026; Chapter III Sections 1, 2 and 3 (the high-risk
   requirements), with the exception of Article 6(5), apply from 2 December 2027 for systems classified high-risk under
   Article 6(2) and Annex III, and from 2 August 2028 for those under Article 6(1) and Annex I; Articles 102 to 110
   apply from 27 July 2026. Point 39 adds that a provider of generative systems placed on the market before
   2 August 2026 must take the steps to meet Article 50(2) by 2 December 2026, and replaces Article 111(2). The
   original dates for the other parts of Article 113 were not shown as changed in the points read.
5. **Not every Omnibus change is about dates.** The act as read also replaces Article 4 (AI literacy), inserts new
   prohibitions at Article 5(1) (ba) and (bb), adds paragraphs 1a to 1c to Article 6 on safety components, and
   replaces Article 50(7) on codes of practice. See parts 2 and 3. Own guidance: re-reading the dates and missing these
   is the same failure as quoting the dates from memory.
6. **Establish the status in the session, every time.** Own guidance: before relying on a date, open the official
   text, note the act number, its publication date and whether a corrigendum or a further amending act exists. The
   Legislative Observatory file and EUR-Lex search for the regulation are the places read here; a consolidated text
   was not read and may lag.
7. **Say what you read and when.** Own guidance: in the readiness note write "Article 113 as amended by Regulation
   (EU) 2026/1744, read on <date>, re-read before relying", never a bare date.
8. **Guidance and standards were not read.** The Commission was to publish guidelines on Article 6 and on Article 50;
   whether they exist, and what they say, were not checked. Do not quote a guideline without opening it.
9. **What this part cannot settle.** Whether an act published after the one read changed any date, how a national
   authority applies a date, and what a transitional rule means for a particular system are for counsel. Not legal
   advice, escalate to counsel.
