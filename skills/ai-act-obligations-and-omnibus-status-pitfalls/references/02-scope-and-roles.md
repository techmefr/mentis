# ai-act-obligations-and-omnibus-status-pitfalls §2 — Scope and roles

> Part 2 of `skills/ai-act-obligations-and-omnibus-status-pitfalls`. Read it when someone asks whether the regulation
> applies or who bears a duty. Source: Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Articles 2, 3(1) and (3) to (7), 4, 25(1),
> as originally published, https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32024R1689 , read
> 2026-10-19; amendments by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 read for Articles 2, 3 and 4 only (points 2 to 5). Statements are
> in our own words. Sentences marked "own guidance" are ours.

1. **The regulation covers several operator types (Article 2(1)).** Providers placing AI systems on the market or
   putting them into service in the Union, or placing general-purpose AI models on it; deployers established or located
   in the Union; providers and deployers in a third country where the output is used in the Union; importers and
   distributors; product manufacturers shipping an AI system with their product under their own name; authorised
   representatives; and affected persons located in the Union. Own guidance: being established outside the Union is not a
   reason to stop the analysis.
2. **Role definitions (Article 3).** A provider develops an AI system or model, or has one developed, and places it on
   the market or puts it into service under its own name or trademark, for payment or free. A deployer uses an AI
   system under its authority, except for personal non-professional use. An importer is established in the Union and
   places on the market a system bearing the name of someone established in a third country. A distributor is in the
   supply chain, is neither provider nor importer, and makes a system available on the Union market.
3. **A role can move (Article 25(1)).** Where a distributor, importer, deployer or other third party puts its name on a
   high-risk system already on the market, substantially modifies one so that it stays high-risk, or changes the
   intended purpose of a non-high-risk system so that it becomes high-risk, it is treated as the provider of that
   high-risk system. Contract terms allocating the duties otherwise are expressly not affected for the naming case.
   Own guidance: rebranding, fine-tuning for a new use or a new customer type are events to record, each one a
   question for counsel.
4. **Intended purpose is defined by what the provider says (Article 3(12)).** It is the use the provider intended,
   including context and conditions, as stated in the instructions, promotional or sales material and technical
   documentation. Own guidance: marketing copy is therefore evidence; keep the copy in the inventory.
5. **A system is defined by inference (Article 3(1)).** A machine-based system designed to operate with varying
   autonomy that infers from input how to generate outputs such as predictions, content, recommendations or decisions.
   Own guidance: whether a given component meets that definition is for counsel when it is not obvious.
6. **Exclusions exist and are narrow as written (Article 2(3) to (12)).** Read: military, defence and national
   security use; systems and models developed and put into service for the sole purpose of scientific research;
   research, testing or development before placing on the market or putting into service, but not testing in real
   world conditions; purely personal non-professional use by natural persons; and systems under free and open-source
   licences unless placed on the market as high-risk or as a system under Article 5 or 50. Own guidance: never rely on
   an exclusion without counsel's written answer, and record the date.
7. **Data protection law continues to apply (Article 2(7)).** The regulation does not displace GDPR; the act read
   adjusts the wording to mention a new Article 4a. Own guidance: an AI inventory and a records-of-processing register
   are two lists to keep consistent, see `skills/gdpr-breach-notification-and-dpia-pitfalls` §3 for the assessment
   side.
8. **AI literacy (Article 4).** As originally published, providers and deployers had to take measures to ensure, to
   their best extent, a sufficient level of AI literacy of staff and others operating the systems. The act read replaces
   Article 4: providers and deployers must take measures to support the development of literacy, without guaranteeing a
   specific level of any individual, while the Commission and the Member States support the effort. Own guidance:
   keep a record of what training was offered to whom, and re-read the article, since a duty can be reworded again.
9. **Questions to put to counsel.** Own guidance: which role applies per system; whether Article 25(1) was triggered
   by a rebrand, a fine-tune or a change of purpose; whether an exclusion in Article 2 applies; whether the Union is
   the place of use; and who in each member state is the competent authority. Not legal advice, escalate to counsel.
