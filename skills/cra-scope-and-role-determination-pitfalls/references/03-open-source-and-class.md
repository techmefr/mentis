# cra-scope-and-role-determination-pitfalls §3 — Open source, non-commercial supply and the class question

> Part 3 of `skills/cra-scope-and-role-determination-pitfalls`. Read it when open-source software is published,
> consumed or maintained, or when the product category is asked about. Source: Regulation (EU) 2024/2847 as
> published in the Official Journal, https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32024R2847,
> read 2026-10-19, original publication, not consolidated: re-read before relying. "Own guidance" marks our
> sentences. Not legal advice, escalate to counsel.

1. **Free of charge is not the same as non-commercial.** The manufacturer definition in Article 3(13) covers
   products marketed "for payment, monetisation or free of charge", while Article 3(22) ties making available to
   a commercial activity. Own guidance: for each published project record whether any commercial activity
   surrounds it (a paid edition, paid support, a hosted offer, a company employing the maintainers for it).
   Whether that makes it a product on the market is counsel's call.
2. **Using open source: the integrator carries diligence.** Article 13(5) requires due diligence when
   integrating third-party components, including free and open-source components not made available on the
   market in the course of a commercial activity. Article 13(6) requires reporting a vulnerability found in a
   component to whoever makes or maintains it and sharing any fix developed where appropriate. Own guidance:
   the inventory (part 4) lists open-source dependencies as a component with an upstream contact.
3. **Publishing open source: the steward question.** Article 3(14) defines an open-source software steward as
   a legal person, other than a manufacturer, whose purpose is to provide sustained support for the
   development of specific products qualifying as free and open-source software and intended for commercial
   activities, and who ensures their viability. Article 3(48) defines free and open-source software by openly
   shared source and a licence granting rights to access, use, modify and redistribute freely. Own guidance,
   flagged for counsel: whether a foundation, a company sponsoring a project, or a team maintaining an
   internal project it also publishes is a steward, a manufacturer, or neither.
4. **Steward duties, as read.** Article 24(1): a documented, verifiable cybersecurity policy fostering secure
   development and vulnerability handling, including voluntary reporting. Article 24(2): cooperation with market
   surveillance authorities and handing over that documentation on reasoned request. Article 24(3): Article
   14(1) applies to the extent the steward is involved in development, and Article 14(3) and (8) to the extent
   severe incidents affect systems the steward provides for development. Re-read before relying.
5. **Voluntary attestation exists.** Article 25 lets the Commission, by delegated act, set up voluntary security
   attestation programmes for free and open-source products, to facilitate Article 13(5) diligence. Whether any
   programme has been established was not checked.
6. **The class question: names only.** Article 7(1) treats products with the core functionality of a category
   set out in Annex III as important products, subject to the conformity assessment procedures of Article 32(2)
   and (3); integrating such a product does not by itself make the host product important. Article 7(2) divides
   Annex III into classes I and II. Article 8 empowers the Commission to require a European cybersecurity
   certificate for categories in Annex IV. Own guidance: the annex lists were not read for this block; record
   the candidate category and the question "does a listed category match its core functionality", and send it to
   counsel with the Annex text read at that time. A product matching no listed category is the default case.
7. **Stop and escalate** on any open-source publication with a company behind it, any sponsored project, and any
   product whose core function sounds like authentication, network protection or a central system function.
