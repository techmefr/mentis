# cra-scope-and-role-determination-pitfalls §1 — Does the Regulation apply to this thing

> Part 1 of `skills/cra-scope-and-role-determination-pitfalls`. Read it when someone asks "is this in scope".
> Source: Regulation (EU) 2024/2847 as published in the Official Journal,
> https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32024R2847, read 2026-10-19. That is the
> original publication, not a consolidated or corrected version: re-read the current text before relying on it.
> Sentences marked "own guidance" are ours. Not legal advice, escalate to counsel.

1. **The test starts from the thing, not the company.** Article 2(1) applies the Regulation to products with
   digital elements made available on the market whose intended purpose or reasonably foreseeable use includes a
   direct or indirect logical or physical data connection to a device or network. Re-read before relying. Own
   guidance: the scope question is asked once per product, with the product named, never once per organisation.
2. **What counts as a product.** Article 3(1) defines a product with digital elements as a software or hardware
   product and its remote data processing solutions, including components placed on the market separately.
   Article 3(2) says remote data processing is processing at a distance for which the software is designed and
   developed by the manufacturer, or under its responsibility, and without which the product could not perform
   one of its functions. Own guidance: an application and the back end it cannot work without are inventoried
   together as one candidate; a back end that is a separate service nobody needs to run the product is a
   separate question for counsel.
3. **The connection wording is wide.** Article 3(8) to (10) define logical, physical and indirect connection
   (indirect: a connection as part of a larger system that is directly connectable). Own guidance: do not conclude
   "offline, so out" from engineering's sense of "connected"; write what interfaces exist and let counsel read
   the definitions against them.
4. **Intended purpose and foreseeable use come from what we said.** Article 3(23) takes the intended purpose from
   the manufacturer's instructions, promotional or sales materials and statements and technical documentation;
   Article 3(24) adds use likely to result from reasonably foreseeable behaviour or technical interaction. Own
   guidance: collect those documents for the product; the claim in a sales page is evidence in the scope answer.
5. **"On the market" is commercial, not only paid.** Article 3(22) defines making available as supply for
   distribution or use in the course of a commercial activity, in return for payment or free of charge; Article
   3(21) defines placing on the market as the first making available on the Union market. Own guidance: record
   for each product whether, where and since when it is supplied on the Union market, and under which commercial
   activity; a free tier does not by itself settle the question.
6. **Excluded cases are listed; check each by name, do not paraphrase from memory.** As read: Article 2(2) names
   products covered by three other Union acts (Regulations (EU) 2017/745, 2017/746 and 2019/2144); Article 2(3)
   products certified under Regulation (EU) 2018/1139; Article 2(4) equipment within Directive 2014/90/EU; Article
   2(6) spare parts replacing identical components made to the same specifications; Article 2(7) products
   developed or modified exclusively for national security or defence, or specifically designed to process
   classified information. Article 2(5) lets the application be limited or excluded where other Union rules
   address the same risks and give the same or a higher level of protection, with the Commission empowered to
   specify this by delegated act. Own guidance: for each product write which exclusion was considered, the
   sentence read, and who decided; "none applies" is also a recorded decision.
7. **Substantial modification can change the answer.** Article 3(30) defines substantial modification as a
   change after placing on the market that affects compliance with the Part I essential requirements or modifies
   the intended purpose assessed. Own guidance: a major release or a repurposing is a trigger to re-ask the
   scope and role questions, not only the first launch.
8. **Stop and escalate** when two readings of a definition fit the product, when an exclusion might apply, or
   when the answer would change a launch. The block never writes "in scope" or "out of scope" as a conclusion.
