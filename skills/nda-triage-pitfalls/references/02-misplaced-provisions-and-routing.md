# nda-triage-pitfalls §2 — Misplaced provisions and routing

> Section 2 of `skills/nda-triage-pitfalls`. Read it when the screen is done and the result must be routed.
> Sources: the triage-nda skill (commit `33eeda1`) and the NDA checklist of the contract-review skill (commit
> `bca0d2e`), re-read 2026-10-16. Both use a three-colour scale and give timelines; we use plain routes and no
> timelines. Sentences marked "own guidance" are ours.

1. **Look for provisions that do not belong in an NDA.** Both sources flag these: a ban on soliciting employees
   or customers, a ban on competing, exclusivity or a promise not to talk to others, a standstill (the first
   source excepts the context of an acquisition), a clause that lets the receiver use what its people retain in
   memory (a residuals clause), any grant or assignment of intellectual property, and audit rights. The
   second source adds a ban on contact with customers, suppliers or employees and a ban on announcing
   the talks. Each one found is a flag, whatever the playbook says about it elsewhere.
2. **Check whether the document is an NDA at all.** If it carries commercial terms, exclusivity or duties beyond
   confidentiality, the first source says to stop, flag it at once and move to a full contract review. Do not
   approve a commercial agreement under the NDA route.
3. **Three routes, no colours.**
   - **Standard approval**: every question in part 1 answered without a flag, nothing from item 1 found, and
     the organisation's delegation of authority allows that person to sign.
   - **Counsel review**: at least one flag that looks fixable in a single pass (a missing exclusion that can
     simply be added, a definition wider than the playbook prefers, a retention exception absent from the
     return clause).
   - **Full legal review**: anything the playbook calls prohibited, a document that is not really an NDA, a
     misplaced provision from item 1, or a direction that does not fit the business reason.
   The sources define their middle and top levels with numeric thresholds and with examples of "narrow"
   residuals; we do not carry those, and the playbook or counsel decides where a flag sits (own guidance).
4. **When in doubt, route higher.** A question that cannot be answered from the text goes to counsel and is
   not counted as passed (own guidance).
5. **Write what the reviewer needs.** For a route other than standard approval: the person or role who reviews
   if the organisation has defined one, and a summary of each issue with the quoted text, written so that the
   reviewer can act without reading the triage in full (first source).
6. **Do not negotiate by drafting.** Proposing a counter-form (the organisation's own NDA) is a decision for
   counsel; writing counter-terms for the other side is not part of a triage (own guidance, in line with the
   sources' repeated "counsel reviews" line).
7. **State the basis.** The report says whether it used the organisation's playbook or the default questions,
   and which version of the NDA was read.
8. **End with the standing line.** This is a screening for routing, not legal advice, and any item the
   reader is unsure about goes to counsel (both sources end this way).
