# nda-triage-pitfalls §1 — Shape and questions

> Section 1 of `skills/nda-triage-pitfalls`. Read it when an NDA arrives and must be screened. Sources: the
> triage-nda skill of the Anthropic knowledge-work-plugins repository (commit `33eeda1`) and the NDA checklist
> of the contract-review skill in `evolsb/claude-legal-skill` (commit `bca0d2e`), re-read 2026-10-16. The
> exclusions and the other items below are drafting practice described by those sources, not statute: they are
> questions for counsel. Sentences marked "own guidance" are ours.

1. **Identify the shape first.** Is it mutual, or one-way, and in which direction? Is it a document of its own or
   a confidentiality section inside a larger agreement? Then ask whether that shape fits the business reason
   for the exchange (first source): a mutual agreement for an open exploration, a one-way one when only one
   side will disclose. For a section inside a larger agreement, the wider agreement can change the analysis;
   say so and use `skills/contract-review-pitfalls` for the whole.
2. **Find the playbook.** The organisation's written NDA criteria say what is required, what is acceptable
   and what is prohibited. If there is none, say so in the output and use a plain list of defaults labelled
   as defaults (both sources do this). The defaults here are the questions below, not numbers (own guidance):
   a duration or a marking period the sources call "standard" has no cited source and differs between
   them.
3. **Is the definition of confidential information proportionate?** A definition of everything, marked or not,
   is the pattern the first source warns about. Does it exclude what is public and what the receiving party
   developed alone?
4. **If marking is required, can it be done?** Marking of written material, and a written confirmation after an
   oral disclosure, are both ways such a requirement is made unworkable. Look at what the text requires and by
   when, and put the question to the people who will do it.
5. **Are the usual exclusions all present?** The sources list five: information already public without the
   receiving party's fault, information already held before disclosure, information developed independently
   without use of the disclosed material, information received lawfully from a third party, and disclosure
   compelled by law, with notice to the discloser where the law permits it. Report each as present or absent.
   The first source singles out independent development and legal compulsion as the ones whose absence is
   most serious.
6. **Who may receive the information?** Employees who need it, contractors and advisers under duties of the same
   level, affiliates where the purpose requires it. Is a flow-down duty required of those recipients, and
   can it be met?
7. **Standard of care and purpose.** What care is owed (reasonable care, or the care applied to one's own
   information), and is use limited to a stated purpose? Is any obligation impractical in a way the
   organisation could not meet (the first source gives encrypting all communications as an example)?
8. **How long do the duties last, and what survives?** Quote the term and the survival period. Whether a given
   length is acceptable is for the playbook, and whether perpetual duties are acceptable for trade secrets is a
   question for counsel (own guidance).
9. **What happens at the end?** Return or destruction, on termination or on request; whether copies required
   by law, regulation or backup practice may be kept; whether a certificate of destruction is wanted, and
   whether it must be sworn.
10. **What remedies are agreed?** Wording on injunctive relief or irreparable harm; any damages fixed in advance;
    whether the remedies apply equally to both sides in a mutual agreement.
11. **Which law and which forum?** Quote them. Whether the choice is acceptable is a playbook and counsel
    matter; the first source also asks whether the law and the forum are consistent with each other and
    whether the agreement forces arbitration.
12. **Quote or say absent.** Each answer carries the text it rests on, or the statement that nothing in the
    document addresses the question (own guidance, same rule as in `skills/contract-review-pitfalls`).
