# dpa-review-pitfalls §3 — The terms against Article 28

> Section 3 of `skills/dpa-review-pitfalls`. Read it when the term-by-term review is run. The article text was
> read on EUR-Lex on 2026-10-16: Regulation (EU) 2016/679, Article 28, original Official Journal text, OJ L
> 119/1 of 4.5.2016, https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32016R0679 . That is the
> original publication and not a consolidated version; corrigenda and later amendments were not checked, so
> read the current consolidated text before relying on any of it. Only what Article 28 says is stated as law
> below; process advice comes from the dpa-review skill (commit `c225d1f`) and is marked as such. Sentences
> marked "own guidance" are ours. Nothing here applies to a document that is not governed by this Regulation.

1. **Article 28(3) lists what the contract must set out.** It says the processing is governed by a binding
   contract or other legal act that sets out the subject-matter and duration of the processing, its nature and
   purpose, the type of personal data, the categories of data subjects, and the controller's obligations and
   rights. Check that each appears in the document or its annexes.
2. **Article 28(3)(a) to (h) list what the processor must be bound to.** Check each as present, absent, unusual
   or unclear, with the quoted text:
   - (a) processing only on documented instructions of the controller, including as to transfers to a third
     country or an international organisation, unless a legal requirement applies to the processor, in which case
     the processor tells the controller before processing unless that law forbids it on important grounds of
     public interest;
   - (b) staff authorised to process are bound to confidentiality, by commitment or by a statutory duty;
   - (c) the measures required under Article 32;
   - (d) the conditions of paragraphs 2 and 4 for engaging another processor;
   - (e) help to the controller, by suitable technical and organisational measures where possible, in answering
     requests from data subjects under Chapter III;
   - (f) help with the obligations under Articles 32 to 36, given the nature of the processing and the
     information available to the processor;
   - (g) at the controller's choice, deletion or return of all personal data after the end of the services, and
     deletion of copies unless Union or Member State law requires storage;
   - (h) making available all information needed to show compliance with the Article and allowing and
     contributing to audits, including inspections, by the controller or an auditor it mandates.
   Articles 32 to 36 were not read for this block; do not state what they require.
3. **The duty to warn.** Article 28(3), second subparagraph, says that regarding point (h) the processor must
   tell the controller at once if, in its opinion, an instruction breaches the Regulation or other Union or
   Member State data protection provisions. Look for it.
4. **Subprocessing, Article 28(2) and (4).** A processor may not engage another processor without prior specific
   or general written authorisation from the controller; with a general authorisation the processor informs
   the controller of intended additions or replacements so the controller can object. The same data protection
   obligations must be imposed on the other processor by contract or other legal act, and the initial
   processor remains fully liable to the controller for the other's failures. Check the authorisation model, the
   notice and objection mechanism, the flow-down and the liability wording, and ask for the list of
   subprocessors if it is only referred to.
5. **Form, Article 28(9).** The contract or legal act must be in writing, including electronic form.
6. **Code of conduct or certification, Article 28(5).** Adherence to an approved code or certification may be
   used as an element to show sufficient guarantees. It is an element; a document claiming it should say which
   one and be checked (own guidance).
7. **Standard clauses, Article 28(6) to (8).** The contract may be based wholly or partly on standard
   contractual clauses that the Commission or a supervisory authority may lay down, without affecting the
   parties' individual contract. If the document says it uses such clauses, ask which and whether the text matches
   them (own guidance).
8. **A processor that decides purposes and means.** Article 28(10) says that a processor which infringes the
   Regulation by determining the purposes and means is to be considered a controller for that processing. Look
   for clauses letting the supplier use the data for its own purposes (analytics, training, product
   improvement) and flag them for counsel, with the quoted wording.
9. **If we are the processor, do not turn controller duties into defects.** The dpa-review skill's boundary:
   legal basis, notices, consents and the lawfulness of instructions stay with the controller. Check instead
   that instructions are documented, that purposes are limited, that assistance is workable, that audits are
   scoped, that incident and deletion timelines are feasible and that subprocessor flow-down is written. If the
   user is the customer, look at the same terms from the other direction.
10. **Say both when a clause is fine commercially but incomplete legally.** The source tells the reviewer to
    state both findings. Do not merge them into one verdict.
11. **Leave out what was not read.** Breach-notification timing, impact assessments, transfer mechanisms and
    the content of data subject rights are in provisions other than Article 28 and were not read here. Report the
    document's wording on them, mark them for counsel, and state no delay, mechanism or period as law. The
    source's regional playbooks for other jurisdictions were not read either.
