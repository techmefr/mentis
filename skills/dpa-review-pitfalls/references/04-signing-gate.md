# dpa-review-pitfalls §4 — Signing gate

> Section 4 of `skills/dpa-review-pitfalls`. Read it when someone asks to sign, approve, countersign or accept.
> Source: `workflows/signing-gate.md` of the dpa-review skill (commit `c225d1f`), re-read 2026-10-16.
> Sentences marked "own guidance" are ours.

1. **Reviewing is analysis; signing is an act with legal consequences.** The source puts it this way and
   interposes a gate. A question such as "can we sign" is never answered with a yes or a no.
2. **Confirm the basics first.** Our role and its direction; the posture, or the assumptions the user
   authorised; the material that is missing.
3. **List the blockers.** Any finding marked as blocking in the term review blocks signing until the missing
   material, the decision or the order of precedence between documents is settled. Missing key annexes, an
   unknown role and unknown transfer facts are blockers by themselves.
4. **Give one of four postures.** The source offers: ready for counsel and the business to decide; acceptable only
   for low-risk or testing use; negotiate before signing; do not sign until the blockers are resolved. Say
   which, and why, in a sentence.
5. **Answer in a fixed form.**
   - current posture;
   - blocking issues;
   - what must be resolved before signing;
   - what would make it acceptable;
   - who decides (a business owner with authority, the data protection officer, counsel);
   - that a qualified lawyer reviews before signature.
6. **No comfort without facts.** While the role, the key annexes or the transfer facts are unresolved, say that
   a signing view cannot be given. The same holds when the document's source was low confidence in part 1.
7. **A user in a hurry gets the same gate.** An urgent request shortens the review; it does not remove the lawyer
   from the path (own guidance).
