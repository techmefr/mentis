# journal-entry-review-pitfalls §1 — Support and authority

> Section 1 of `skills/journal-entry-review-pitfalls`. Read it when an entry is prepared or an approval matrix
> is drawn. Sourced from the journal-entry-prep skill of anthropics' knowledge-work-plugins (commit `33eeda1`,
> `finance/skills/journal-entry-prep/SKILL.md`), read 2026-10-16, sections on supporting documentation and on
> review and approval. Sentences marked "own guidance" are ours.

1. **Every manual entry carries seven things.** The source lists them: a clear description of what the entry
   records and why; the calculation support (formula, schedule or source data reference); references to the
   underlying events (order, invoice, contract or payroll register numbers); the period it applies to; who
   prepared it and when; evidence of review and approval under the organisation's matrix; and whether it
   reverses, with the reversal date. Own guidance: an entry missing any of them is returned, not fixed by the
   reviewer.
2. **The description is for someone who was not there.** The reviewer checklist asks for a description clear
   and specific enough for audit purposes. Own guidance: write it so a person reading the ledger a year later
   can tell what was recorded and what to look at, without opening the preparer's file.
3. **Calculation support is a pointer, not a number.** Own guidance: cite the schedule, the report or the
   contract clause the amount comes from and the date it was read, so the reviewer repeats the calculation
   instead of trusting a typed total.
4. **A reversal indicator is part of the entry.** The source asks for it on every entry and names a missing
   reversal as a cause of double counting (part 2). Own guidance: decide at preparation, not at review,
   whether an entry reverses and when.
5. **Higher approval for the unusual kinds.** The source's matrix gives routine recurring entries to a manager
   and sends non-recurring manual entries to higher levels as amounts rise, and sends top-side or consolidation
   entries and out-of-period adjustments to a controller or above whatever the amount. Carry the shape: the
   rarer and the more outside the normal flow an entry is, the higher the approver. The source itself says its
   thresholds must come from the organisation's own materiality and risk tolerance, and none is carried here.
6. **Authority is checked, not presumed.** The reviewer confirms the amount is within the preparer's authority
   level. Own guidance: keep the authority list somewhere the reviewer can open, and treat an entry split into
   several smaller ones to stay under a limit as a finding.
7. **What this part does not decide.** How a transaction is accounted for (the source's sections on accruals,
   depreciation, prepaid amortisation, payroll and revenue recognition, including its reference to a
   revenue-recognition standard) was not verified and is not carried; send those questions to an accountant.
