# customer-escalation-pitfalls

Written for the account manager and equally for a support lead. The premise: **an escalation is two problems,
the incident and what it did to the relationship; fixing the first does not fix the second, and the common
failure is going quiet until the answer exists.** One customer is involved here. When an incident affects many
customers at once, read `incident-comms-pitfalls`; for the sorting of one report before it escalates, read
`bug-triage`; for the broader support and account-health process, `business/customer-success`.

## When
- A customer's executive or a more senior contact is now involved, or someone has said they may leave.
- A problem is resolved technically and the customer is still angry.
- You need your own engineering, leadership or another team to act on a customer's behalf, on a deadline.

## Output / checkpoint
Which kind of escalation it is and how you know; the first response written out and sent; what the customer
wants, marked as asked or assumed; the owner, the channel and the cadence; the executive decision and brief;
the internal ask as one sentence; the repair step with a date. Anything you could not check is listed.

## Guardrails
- Never go quiet because you have no answer; send the acknowledgement and a next-update time (§1.2).
- Never treat a relationship escalation as a failure escalation (§1.1).
- Never put a cause theory, an apology for something not established or a fix date you cannot hold in the
  first response (§1.2).
- Never let two people from your side send the customer two versions (§1.4).
- Never bring in an executive without a brief, or only to show urgency (§2.1, §2.2).
- Never read the quiet after resolution as relief (§2.3).
- Never send "can someone look at this"; name the person, the action and the date (§3.2).
- Never inflate severity or claim churn you would not defend (§3.3).
- Never disappear after escalating; escalation is not delegation (§3.4).
- The "first hour" and similar timings are one source's examples, not a standard. The source is a young
  repository (see [`references/origin.md`](./references/origin.md)), so each rule here is kept for its own
  merit and none is presented as established practice.
