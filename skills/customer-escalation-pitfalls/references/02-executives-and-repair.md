# customer-escalation-pitfalls §2 — Executives and repair

Two decisions a good first response does not settle: who else joins, and what happens after the incident
closes. Skipping the second is the usual way a resolved escalation turns into a lost account.

## 2.1 When to bring an executive
1. **Treat bringing your executive in as a card you hold once.** Early, nothing is left when it gets worse;
   late, it reads as reluctance (CSPulse).
2. **Bring one in when**: their executive is already involved and the levels should match; what the customer
   needs committed is above your authority and waiting would take days; or the relationship, not the incident,
   is the problem and the message is that this account matters (CSPulse).
3. **Do not bring one in to demonstrate urgency.** An unbriefed executive makes commitments that you then have
   to unpick (CSPulse).

## 2.2 The brief
1. **Give whoever joins**: the facts, what is unknown, what has been committed, what must not be promised and
   what the customer actually wants (§1.3).
2. **The brief is written, short and sent before the call**, not delivered in the first minutes of it
   (own guidance).

## 2.3 The repair step after the incident closes
1. **The incident closes; the relationship does not close with it.** The pressure comes off, which is why this
   step is skipped.
2. **Say in writing what happened and what changes**, once the cause is known and not merely suspected.
3. **Ask what it cost them.** The answer tells you whether it was an inconvenience or a broken plan with their
   own executive attached, which are different accounts from then on.
4. **Make a specific commitment, meet it visibly and inside a short window**, something they asked for and not
   a gesture.
5. **An escalation surfaces people you had not met**, often including the one who decides. Meet them and record
   them in your account notes.
6. **Do not read quiet cooperation right after resolution as relief.** It can be a decision being made
   elsewhere.
7. **Record the escalation** as an input to the next renewal conversation.

## 2.4 Verify
For the repair: check, a week later, that the specific commitment was met and that the customer has seen it
met, by asking. For the executive decision: check that the person you brought in repeated nothing from the
"must not be promised" list.
