# training-objectives-and-formative-assessment-pitfalls

Not a step of the pipeline: a craft block for people who teach a team something (an onboarding course, an
internal workshop, a tool training). The premise: **an objective that cannot be observed cannot be checked,
and a session that never checks is run on the room's politeness**. For learning exercises inside a coding
session see `learning-mode-exercise-pitfalls`; for how a team talks and records decisions see
`internal-comms-async-pitfalls`.

## When
- Writing the objectives of a session, course or onboarding plan, or reviewing someone else's.
- A session plan lists topics and slides but no way to see whether people got it.
- A session runs behind and the instructor must decide what to cut.
- Collecting feedback during or after a session and acting on it.

## Output / checkpoint
For a session plan, build a three-column table of objective, activity and assessment, and check that no
cell is empty and that each row names the same level of action. Then list where the checkpoints fall and
what question each one asks. Any objective whose verb is "understand" or "know" must be rewritten before
the plan is called done. Nothing here was run with a group.

## Guardrails
- Never write an objective that no one could observe (§1.1).
- Never ask a room "does everyone understand?" and count the silence as a yes (§3.1).
- Never leave an objective without an activity or an assessment, or an activity with no objective (§2.2).
- Never speed up when behind; decide the cuts in advance and tell co-trainers and learners what was cut
  (§3.2).
- Never treat the novice sequencing rule as settled; the two sources disagree and the answer depends on the
  audience (§1.3).
- Frequencies, durations and examples here are single-source choices; nothing in this block is an HR,
  employment or certification rule.
