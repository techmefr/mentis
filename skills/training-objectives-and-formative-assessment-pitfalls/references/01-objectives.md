# training-objectives-and-formative-assessment-pitfalls §1 — Objectives

## 1.1 An objective is an observable action
1. **State what the learner will be able to do.** The idstack skill asks for the learner, an observable
   action, the conditions (context, tools, time) and the standard (accuracy, speed, completeness); not every
   objective needs all four, but the action must always be observable.
2. **"Understand" and "know" are not assessable.** The Carpentries episode says these words are hard to
   assess and unclear about the level of understanding wanted; rewrite them as an outcome that can be
   achieved and checked.
3. **Example from idstack**: "understand the importance of ethics" is not measurable; "evaluate a research
   proposal for ethical compliance using a named guideline" is.
4. **Test an objective two ways** (Carpentries exercise): name a harder objective on the same topic for
   someone who has mastered it, and name one more basic thing a learner who struggles might be missing.

## 1.2 The same verb sits at different levels
1. **A verb alone does not give the level.** idstack says common verbs (analyze, evaluate, explain,
   describe, compare, design, assess, interpret, create and others) can mean different cognitive levels in
   different objectives.
2. **When the verb is ambiguous, ask which meaning is intended** instead of classifying it yourself:
   following a given procedure step by step, or independently breaking material down.
3. **Classify on two axes.** idstack uses the revised Bloom taxonomy: a knowledge axis (factual,
   conceptual, procedural, metacognitive) and a process axis (remember, understand, apply, analyze,
   evaluate, create). Choosing only a verb misses half of this.
4. **The level drives the rest**: the activity and the assessment (§2) are chosen from it, so a wrong level
   spreads.

## 1.3 Novices and sequencing: a judgment call
The two sources disagree. Carry only what they share and decide by audience.
1. **Both want objectives that are specific actions** and both warn against vague verbs (§1.1).
2. **Carpentries** says its objectives target novices with basic verbs such as recognise, distinguish and
   use, and warns that experts who push learners to higher-order tasks too soon skip steps and overload
   them.
3. **idstack** says learners do not have to master facts before higher-order work and that a strict
   low-to-high sequence is worth flagging; it grounds this in an "evidence tier" scheme that was not
   verified here.
4. **Judgment call**: for a mixed or new audience, make lower-level objectives optional or check them with a
   short pre-assessment (an idstack suggestion), and watch the room for overload (Carpentries). Do not
   quote either source as settled.
