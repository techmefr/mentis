# month-end-close-sequencing-pitfalls §1 — Close sequencing

> Section 1 of `skills/month-end-close-sequencing-pitfalls`. Read it when a close is planned, run or shortened.
> Sourced from the close-management skill of anthropics' knowledge-work-plugins (commit `33eeda1`,
> `finance/skills/close-management/SKILL.md`), read 2026-10-16, sections on sequencing, status tracking,
> accelerated close, bottlenecks and retrospective. Sentences marked "own guidance" are ours.

## Order by dependency
1. **A close is a dependency graph, not a checklist.** The source arranges tasks in levels by what must be
   finished first: recording of cash and payables and receivables, payroll, recurring entries, accruals and
   intercompany postings come first; then bank and subledger reconciliations, revenue entries and foreign
   currency revaluation; then the full set of account reconciliations, the entries they reveal and a
   preliminary trial balance; then draft statements and a first variance review; then management review,
   final adjustments, the lock and the reporting package. Carry the order: entries and cut-off, then
   reconciliations, then adjustments, then draft statements, then review, then lock.
2. **A reconciliation needs final inputs.** The source's own examples: the bank reconciliation needs the cash
   entries and the statement; the account reconciliations need all entries posted. Own guidance: a
   reconciliation done before its inputs are final will be redone, so the plan names each task's inputs.
3. **Name the critical path.** The chain of dependent tasks sets the minimum duration of the close. Own
   guidance: draw it for your own close, because it is not the same in every organisation.

## Shorten it where the work is
4. **Automate the recurring entries** (the source's examples: depreciation, amortisation, standard accruals)
   and **reconcile continuously during the month** instead of all at once afterwards.
5. **Run independent reconciliations in parallel,** set clear deadlines, and use standard templates.
6. **Prepare before the period ends.** The source lists cut-off confirmation, a preliminary bank
   reconciliation and a review of open orders for accrual needs as pre-close work. Its prerequisites for a
   much faster close are automation, continuous reconciliation, pre-close preparation and clear ownership with
   few handoffs; its target durations are goals of one author and are not carried.

## Soft close and hard close
7. **Two states, defined in advance.** Soft close: all entries are final, review and draft statements remain.
   Hard close: statements are final and the period is locked in the system. The source's flow ends with
   adjustments from management review, then the lock, then the package to stakeholders. Own guidance: write
   down who may post between the two and how a late item is handled, because that gap is where entries sneak in.
8. **Whether a period lock is legally required** was not read; ask your accountant.

## Track every task
9. **Each task has an owner, a deadline, a status and, if blocked, the blocker.** The five statuses in the
   source: not started (possibly waiting on a dependency), in progress, complete (finished and reviewed),
   blocked, at risk (in progress and may miss its deadline).
10. **A short daily status review during the close:** walk the open tasks, name the blockers, reassign or
    escalate, and re-estimate the timeline if any task is at risk.
11. **Metrics to follow over time:** days from period end to hard close, entries posted after soft close, late
    tasks, reconciliation exceptions needing investigation, and corrections found after close. The source
    gives directions (fewer, shorter) and no figures; none is added here.

## Learn from each close
12. **Hold a retrospective every time,** with the source's five questions in substance: what to keep, what took
    longer and why, what blocked and how to prevent it, which result surprised us that we should have caught
    earlier, and what can be automated.
13. **Typical bottlenecks and their fixes** in the source: late payables accruals (continuous estimation and a
    cut-off deadline), manual recurring entries (automate), slow reconciliations (rolling reconciliation),
    intercompany delays (automated matching and firm deadlines), large changes at management review (a better
    preliminary review), missing documents (keep them during the month).
