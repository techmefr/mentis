# e-invoicing-obligations-pitfalls §2 — The national layer and the dated reading

> Part 2 of `skills/e-invoicing-obligations-pitfalls`. Read it when a date, a mandate or a platform duty is about to
> enter a plan. Sources: Directive (EU) 2025/516 (CELEX:32025L0516), read 2026-10-19, and the French tax
> administration page https://www.impots.gouv.fr/professionnel/je-passe-la-facturation-electronique , read
> 2026-10-19 (page said "published 16/10/2024, modified 01/09/2026"). Method for official text:
> `business/regulatory-watch` §5. Own guidance sentences are ours. Not tax or legal advice.

1. **The Directive leaves the national rules to the Member States.** Its Article 6 sets for each group of
   amendments a date by which Member States adopt and publish measures and a date from which they apply them
   (31 December 2026 and 1 January 2027 for Article 2, 30 June 2030 and 1 July 2030 for Article 5, and others),
   and Article 6(6) and (7) require national measures to refer to the Directive and be communicated to the
   Commission. Own guidance: what binds the company is the national measure, so the plan starts from the national
   authority's page, not from the EU text. Re-read before relying.
2. **Find the authority's own page, not a summary of it.** Own guidance: the first source for each country is the tax
   administration's e-invoicing page, found from its professional section; then the legal documentation it links.
   Vendors, consultancies and press are leads only. Write down the URL, the "last modified" date shown on the page
   and the date you read it.
3. **A URL guessed from memory may not exist.** Own observation, 2026-10-19: two plausible addresses on the French
   site returned "page not found"; the working page was reached from the professional section's links. Own guidance:
   navigate from the authority's home page and record the path you took.
4. **Example of the national trap, France, read 2026-10-19 (re-read before relying).** The page says it informs
   about the generalisation of e-invoicing between businesses and of data transmission to the administration, "effective
   since 1 September 2026"; it lists a practical guide (PDF), a calendar PDF, a list of platforms approved by the
   administration and a legal documentation PDF, and carries seven questions dated 16 January 2026 (for instance
   whether an invoice sent by email is an electronic invoice, how to proceed with mixed business and consumer
   customers, and from when a company is concerned). Their answers, the guide, the calendar and the approved
   platform list were not read. Own guidance: that page is a pointer, not a rule set; the dates by company size,
   the duties to issue versus receive, and the platform requirements are in the documents it links, to be read and
   dated by whoever relies on them.
5. **The EU text and the national page do not use the same words.** The Directive speaks of electronic invoices and
   digital reporting; the French page speaks of e-invoicing and transmission of data to the administration.
   Own guidance: keep a glossary line per country mapping the national terms to the Directive terms, and never
   translate a national duty into "the EU rule".
6. **Every national statement carries three tags.** Own guidance: URL, date read, "re-read before relying". A
   statement in a plan or ticket without the three is a rumour. Re-read the source on a schedule
   (`skills/source-freshness`) and whenever an adviser or customer quotes a different date.
7. **Do not state a national duty you did not read.** This block states no French date, threshold, format or
   platform requirement other than the page statements in point 4. Own guidance: for any other country, repeat
   this reading on that authority's page and write the country's own record.
8. **Cross-border flows touch two countries.** Own guidance: for a flow between two Member States, read both
   authorities' pages and the Directive's reporting provisions for intra-Community transactions (part 1, point 8),
   and ask the adviser which country's rule applies to issuing and to reporting.
