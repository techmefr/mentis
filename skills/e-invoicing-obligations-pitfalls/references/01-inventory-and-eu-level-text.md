# e-invoicing-obligations-pitfalls §1 — Inventory and the EU-level text

> Part 1 of `skills/e-invoicing-obligations-pitfalls`. Read it when the invoice flows are not listed or someone
> quotes "the EU rule". Source: Council Directive (EU) 2025/516 of 11 March 2025 amending Directive 2006/112/EC as
> regards VAT rules for the digital age, OJ L of 25.3.2025, https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32025L0516 ,
> English version, read 2026-10-19 (original publication, not a consolidated text). Statements marked "re-read
> before relying". Sentences marked "own guidance" are ours. Not tax or legal advice: the accountant, the tax
> adviser and the national authority decide.

1. **List every invoice flow before reading any rule.** Own guidance: one row per flow, with issuer, recipient type
   (business, consumer, public body), country, system that produces it, how it leaves (email, portal, network),
   how it is received, volume per month and a named owner. Flows people forget: credit notes and corrective
   invoices, self-billing, reverse-charge invoices, invoices issued by a marketplace on behalf of sellers, and
   invoices received from suppliers.
2. **The Directive amends the VAT Directive; it is not a national law.** It is addressed to Member States (its
   Article 8) and changes Directive 2006/112/EC. Whatever a company must do is written in the law of the country
   concerned. Re-read before relying.
3. **The amended definition of an electronic invoice is about structure, not about transport.** Amended Article 217
   says an electronic invoice is one containing the required information and issued, transmitted and received in a
   structured electronic format that allows automated and electronic processing, at least for the data referred to
   in Articles 262 and 271b. Own guidance: a PDF sent by email is not shown by that wording to qualify; the
   question "does our format count" goes to the adviser, never answered by the team. Re-read before relying.
4. **The default becomes electronic, with an opening for Member States.** Amended Article 218(2) says invoices shall
   be issued as electronic invoices, while Member States may accept paper or other electronic formats for
   transactions not subject to the reporting obligations of Chapter 6. This amended Article 218 sits in Article 5
   of the Directive, applying from 1 July 2030 (Article 6(5)). Own guidance: mark every date by the Article that
   carries it, since the Directive has several dates. Re-read before relying.
5. **A standard is named, not copied.** Amended Article 218(3) requires electronic invoices to comply with the
   European standard on electronic invoicing and the list of its syntaxes under Directive 2014/55/EU; Member States
   may allow other standards for supplies within their territory other than those in Article 262. A footnote of the
   Directive refers to Commission Implementing Decision (EU) 2017/1870 for the publication of that reference. Own
   guidance: the standard is EN 16931; this block names it and does not restate it, since its text was not read
   here. Ask the vendor or adviser which syntaxes and which national extensions apply to each flow.
6. **A Member State can mandate domestic e-invoicing earlier.** Article 1(2) of the Directive adds to Article 218 a
   paragraph letting Member States require taxable persons established in their territory to issue electronic
   invoices for supplies within their territory (other than those in Article 262), under conditions they lay down.
   Article 6(1) lets Member States apply that from 14 April 2025. Own guidance: a national mandate can therefore
   exist before the EU default applies; do not wait for 2030. Re-read before relying.
7. **Acceptance by the recipient is touched.** Article 1(3) lets Member States that use that option provide that
   issuing an electronic invoice does not depend on the recipient's acceptance. Amended Article 232 (Article 5(9)) says
   issuing a standard-compliant electronic invoice shall not depend on acceptance, with a Member State option for
   transactions outside Chapter 6 reporting. Own guidance: put "does the customer have to accept it" as a line in
   the inventory for each flow and ask the adviser.
8. **Digital reporting is a second duty, separate from the invoice.** Article 5(15) to (17) replace Articles 262
   to 264: intra-Community supplies and acquisitions and certain reverse-charge transactions require data to be
   submitted per transaction; Article 263 sets timing tied to the issue of the invoice, or five days after issue
   or receipt in the cases it lists; Article 264 lists the invoice data points to send. These apply from
   1 July 2030 (Article 6(5)). Own guidance: record which flows are cross-border and which are domestic, since the
   duties differ. Re-read before relying.
9. **Domestic reporting is an option for Member States.** New Articles 271a and 271b let Member States require
   taxable persons to send transaction data to their tax authorities for domestic supplies, transmitted when the
   invoice is issued or within five days after issue or receipt in listed cases, and allow transmission from
   standard-compliant electronic invoices. Member States may limit the obligation to categories of taxable person or
   types of transaction and decide the data to send (271b(4)). Own guidance: this is exactly where national rules
   diverge; part 2 explains how to find the current one.
10. **Some Member States get a later date.** Article 6(5), second subparagraph, gives states that already had a
    domestic real-time transaction-based reporting obligation (or authorisation or legislation) before
    1 January 2024 until 1 January 2035 for the measures on Article 218 and Articles 271a and 271b, as far as domestic
    e-invoicing and reporting are concerned, and the Commission may propose postponement if its report shows
    shortcomings. Own guidance: never read one date and assume it applies to every country in the inventory.
11. **New mandatory invoice content.** Amended Article 226 adds the sequential number of the corrected invoice for a
    corrective invoice, and bank account numbers or identifiers of the supplier's accounts for payment (points 16
    and 17). Own guidance: check the billing template against the national list; the Directive lists content for
    the EU frame only.
12. **Deduction can be tied to holding an electronic invoice.** Article 5(3) lets Member States provide that, where
    domestic reporting applies, the customer may deduct VAT only if it holds an electronic invoice issued under
    Article 218(3). Own guidance: this is a customer-side risk for a supplier; a refusal to issue a compliant
    invoice may reach the customer's deduction.
13. **Archiving and authenticity are separate lines.** Amended Article 233(2) still lists technologies for
    authenticity of origin and integrity of content as examples. Own guidance: archiving duration, location and
    format rules were not read here; the inventory carries a line "where invoices are stored, in which form, for
    how long, according to whom", answered by the adviser.
14. **The Directive plans its own review.** Article 271c asks the Commission for an interim evaluation report by
    31 March 2033. Own guidance: the frame can still move, which is a reason to date every statement.
