# okr-scoring-pitfalls §2 — Integrity and evidence

After the type is fixed, the remaining failures are about what is allowed to change and what a score is
allowed to mean.

## 2.1 Targets and scope are frozen
1. **No retroactive change** of baseline, target or key result definition (product-on-purpose). "We hit it
   because we changed the target" is the pattern.
2. **If the target moved mid-cycle, say so and grade against both** the original and the adjusted version
   (product-on-purpose).
3. **No retroactive shrinking** of a committed or compliance key result to mark partial coverage as a pass;
   see reference 01, deferred states.
4. **A stakeholder's preferred framing does not override the evidence**; the grader's read is independent of
   it (product-on-purpose).

## 2.2 Effort is not impact
1. **Keep ship status and key result impact in separate columns** (product-on-purpose). An initiative that
   shipped on time and did not move its key result is not a partial win.
2. **Treat initiatives as bets, not commitments**: the source borrows this framing from Castro. For each,
   say which key result it was meant to move, whether it shipped, its apparent contribution and whether the
   evidence supports continuing, retiring or reworking it.
3. **Do not claim an initiative drove a key result** when timing or instrumentation cannot support the claim;
   write "apparent contribution" and mark it unclear (product-on-purpose).

## 2.3 Evidence confidence
1. **Every score carries a confidence**: high, medium, low or unknown (product-on-purpose).
2. **Name the issue behind a low one**: instrumentation gaps, target shifts, a changed cohort definition,
   a measurement window that does not match the objective, a small sample. Each gets its impact on the score
   and a fix for the next measurement plan.
3. **Precision hides weakness**: a two-decimal score on weak evidence misleads; surface the confidence
   instead of letting the number imply certainty (product-on-purpose).
4. **A risks-in-interpretation list** of places the score could mislead belongs in the review itself
   (product-on-purpose's template).

## 2.4 Sandbagging and misuse for individuals
1. **Flag sandbagging**: consistently scoring above 0.85 on aspirational targets is a sign the targets were
   set too low, to be recalibrated next cycle (product-on-purpose; the threshold is the source's).
2. **OKR scores are not individual ratings or pay inputs.** The source refuses the request and names two
   risks: sandbagging and the suppression of honest learning. If someone asks, say that and stop.
3. **The review is not the system of record.** Point to the live tracker from the review
   (product-on-purpose's source-of-truth field), so a stale copy is not mistaken for the record.
4. **Route what was learned**: product or customer learning forward to the next cycle, process learning to a
   retrospective, measurement learning to whoever owns the instrumentation (product-on-purpose).
